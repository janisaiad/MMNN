\begin{abstract}
We study \textit{Multi-Component Multi-layer Neural Networks} (MMNN), an architecture where only the linear outputs $(A^{(\ell)},c^{(\ell)})$ are trained while the input weights $(w^{(\ell)},b^{(\ell)})$ are frozen at random. This factorization drastically reduces the optimization dimension, stabilizes training under NTK parameterization, and admits an explicit Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) recursion in the sequential infinite-width limit. We formalize the MMNN model, derive the NTK recursion, discuss the base and derivative NNGP kernels, and explain how low-rank bottlenecks yield fast $O(1/r)$ fluctuation decay. We connect these properties to emergent scaling laws and to the extensive-rank viewpoint where random features and low-rank structure jointly precondition optimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We consider MMNN as a principled compromise between expressivity and stability: at each layer $\ell$, the network linearly combines a frozen random nonlinear basis, and only the output weights and biases are trained. Empirically, MMNN exhibits dictionary/wavelet-like feature selection and ``spike'' localization via ReLU gating, while theoretically it supports a kernel regime governed by an explicit NTK recursion. Two complementary regimes arise:
(i) NTK parameterization ($1/\sqrt{n_\ell}$) inducing lazy training and linearized dynamics,
(ii) mean-field parameterization fostering feature learning with nonlinear parameter evolution.
Our focus is on the NTK view, its spectral preconditioning, and its interaction with low-rank bottlenecks.

\section{MMNN Model}
Let $\bm{h}^{(0)}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_0}$. For $\ell=1,\dots,L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mmnn}
\bm{h}^{(\ell)}(\bm{x})
= \frac{\sigma_A}{\sqrt{n_\ell}} \sum_{j=1}^{n_\ell} \bm{A}^{(\ell)}_j\,
\ReLU\!\Big(\bm{w}^{(\ell)\top}_j\,\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(\bm{x})+\beta\,b^{(\ell)}_j\Big)
+ \sigma_c\,\bm{c}^{(\ell)}.
\end{equation}
Training policy: $(\bm{A}^{(\ell)},\bm{c}^{(\ell)})$ are trained; $(\bm{w}^{(\ell)},b^{(\ell)})$ are frozen random draws. The $1/\sqrt{n_\ell}$ scaling (NTK parameterization) ensures a well-defined infinite-width limit; $\beta$ controls internal biases; $\sigma_c$ scales output biases. Low-rank bottlenecks (intermediate dimension $r\ll n_\ell$) compress correlations and improve conditioning.

\section{Base NNGP and Derivative Kernels}
In the sequential infinite-width limit, the layer-$\ell$ base kernel is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{(\ell)}(x,x')
= \mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},b}\Big[\ReLU\!\big(\bm{w}^\top \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x)+\beta b\big)\,\ReLU\!\big(\bm{w}^\top \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x')+\beta b\big)\Big],
\end{equation}
and the derivative kernel is
\begin{equation}
\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}(x,x')
= \mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},b}\Big[\dot{\ReLU}\!\big(\bm{w}^\top \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x)+\beta b\big)\,\dot{\ReLU}\!\big(\bm{w}^\top \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x')+\beta b\big)\;\bm{w}^\top\bm{w}\Big].
\end{equation}
For $\ell=1$ and centered Gaussian $(\bm{w},b)$, $\Sigma^{(1)}$ is rotation-invariant with standard closed forms, e.g. in terms of input cosine similarity $\rho$.

\section{NTK Recursion for MMNN}
\begin{theorem}[NTK recursion]
Under NTK parameterization and a sequential limit $n_1\to\infty,\dots,n_L\to\infty$ with $(\bm{w}^{(\ell)},b^{(\ell)})$ i.i.d. frozen after initialization, the layer-$\ell$ NTK satisfies
\begin{align}
\Kop^{(0)}(x,x') \;&=\; 0, \\
\Kop^{(\ell)}(x,x') \;&=\; \Kop^{(\ell-1)}(x,x')\cdot\big(\sigma_A^2\,\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}(x,x')\big)\;+\;\sigma_A^2\,\Sigma^{(\ell)}(x,x')\;+\;\sigma_c^2,\quad \ell\ge1.
\end{align}
For $\ell=1$, $\Sigma^{(1)}$ is deterministic; for $\ell>1$, $\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ and $\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}$ are random fields whose fluctuations shrink with width and with the rank $r$ of bottlenecks.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The additive term $\sigma_A^2\Sigma^{(\ell)}$ encodes fresh basis contribution at layer $\ell$, while the multiplicative coupling $\Kop^{(\ell-1)}\cdot(\sigma_A^2\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)})$ transports geometry through layers. The bias term $\sigma_c^2$ (often set to $1$) stabilizes the kernel baseline.

\section{Low-Rank Bottlenecks and $O(1/r)$ Fluctuations}
Low-rank projections concentrate inner products and reduce kernel randomness. In particular, when an intermediate representation is compressed to rank $r$, effective correlations concentrate with variance $O(1/r)$, yielding:
\begin{proposition}[Fast fluctuation decay]
Let $\tilde{\Kop}^{(\ell)}$ denote the deterministic (zonal) limit of $\Kop^{(\ell)}$ induced by the low-rank layer. Then, for moderate $r$,
\[
\big\|\Kop^{(\ell)} - \tilde{\Kop}^{(\ell)}\big\| = O\!\left(\frac{1}{r}\right),
\]
so spectral analysis can be approximated by the deterministic kernel operator for practical $r$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Scaling Laws and Extensive-Rank Viewpoint}
In an extensive-rank regime (e.g., $r\simeq d^\beta$), training curves may exhibit staircase-like phases consistent with additive model activation of latent directions. The MMNN NTK captures early lazy-training behavior with a reduced parameter budget due to low-rank structure, suggesting improved conditioning and faster initial convergence compared to standard MLPs, while preserving expressivity through the random-feature RKHS induced by the frozen bases.

\section{Discussion and Outlook}
MMNN merges random features with low-rank structure to produce a tractable NTK recursion and favorable optimization preconditioning. Future directions include:
(i) fine spectral analysis of the zonal kernel and finite-width corrections,
(ii) quantitative fluctuation bounds vs. $r$ and depth,
(iii) empirical scaling-law validation under increasing $(d,N,r)$ with controlled cosine geometry,
(iv) extensions beyond Gaussian pre-activations and bias control via $\beta$.
