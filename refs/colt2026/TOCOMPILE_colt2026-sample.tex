\begin{abstract}
We study low-rank random-feature networks (RF-LR), where each layer is a frozen random feature map followed by a trainable readout into a bottleneck of dimension \(r\ll N\).
We focus on a finite-sample kernel regime: we take a sequential infinite-width limit \(N\to\infty\) at fixed dataset size \(n\), and then study how the remaining bottleneck randomness concentrates as \(r\to\infty\).

Our main results quantify how depth \(L\) and rank \(r\) control conditioning and optimization scales in kernel regression. In an exactly solvable equicorrelated dataset model we obtain an explicit Gram spectrum, with a nontrivial scale \(\Theta(1/(rL))\) on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\), yielding a kernel time scale \(\Theta(P/n)\) under \(P\asymp LrN\) and \(N\asymp n^2\). We also derive an explicit RF-LR NTK recursion and show, as a secondary consequence, that the mean three-layer kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel via an endpoint (Puiseux) expansion.
We do not analyze the empirical RF-LR NTK in the quadratic random-matrix scaling \(n\to\infty\); adapting resolvent-based bulk-law techniques developed for shallow two-layer NTKs~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent} to low-rank RF-LR and deeper architectures is left for future work.
\end{abstract}
