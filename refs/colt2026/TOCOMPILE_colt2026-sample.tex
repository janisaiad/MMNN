\begin{abstract}
We develop a Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory for low-rank random feature networks (RF-LR) that provides a principled, computationally efficient route to the kernel regime. Assuming fixed weights as classical NTK theory predicts, we prove that RF-LR preserves the same reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) as the shallow ReLU kernel; low-rank weight matrices do not lose expressivity. Combining Fisher--Kibble decoupling of Gaussian process outputs with a hypergeometric analysis of the mean NTK near \( \rho=1 \), we establish the Puiseux expansion and RKHS equivalence. We link the \( I(r) \sim 1/\sqrt{r} \) decay to tighter concentration as \( r \) grows. The empirical three-layer NTK concentrates around its deterministic limit with sub-Gaussian tails in \( r \). At the spectral level, the NTK Gram matrix exhibits a spiked and shifted Marchenko--Pastur law. We give an explicit NTK recursion and closed-form depth expansion. Taken together, these results establish that RF-LR preserves expressivity and gives optimization guarantees while lowering the parameter cost from \( O(N^2) \) to \( O(rN) \).
\end{abstract}
