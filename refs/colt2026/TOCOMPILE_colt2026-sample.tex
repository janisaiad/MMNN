\begin{abstract}
Deep networks with low-rank bottlenecks (RF-LR) drastically cut trainable parameters by freezing random feature maps and training only narrow readouts of dimension \(r\ll N\) per layer. In the lazy/NTK regime, training reduces to kernel regression, so the spectrum of the neural tangent kernel controls convergence and stability. We study how depth \(L\) and bottleneck rank \(r\) shape this kernel: we take the sequential infinite-width limit \(N\to\infty\), then analyze how the remaining randomness concentrates as \(r\to\infty\).

We give an explicit \emph{new} NTK recursion for RF-LR with a visible \(1/r\) factor at each bottleneck layer and a closed-form expansion at any depth. For the deterministic mean (proxy) kernel we prove sharp depth scaling: correlations align to \(1\) at rate \(O(k^{-2})\) (same as for MLPs at the edge of chaos), the kernel magnitude saturates, and the diagonal--off-diagonal gap decays as \(\asymp 1/(rk)\). We prove a condition-number lower bound \(\kappa\ge\Omega(r\cdot L)\) for the proxy Gram matrix in general, and exact conditioning \(\kappa_\perp=1\) or \(\kappa_\perp=1+o(1)\) for equicorrelated or high-dimensional spherical data (full proof in the appendix). Under a fixed parameter budget \(O(NLr)\), depth and rank trade off; rank and depth commute from a conditioning perspective. For the three-layer network we show that \emph{low rank does not shrink the kernel function class}: the mean RF-LR kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel via a non-integer Taylor series at the endpoint, so expressivity is preserved while trainable parameters drop from \(O(LN^2)\) to \(O(LrN)\). We link the \(I(r)\sim 1/\sqrt{r}\) scaling to \(1/\sqrt{r}\) sub-Gaussian concentration of the empirical kernel around the proxy and prove a rigorous proxy--empirical bound (Appendix~\ref{appendix:proxy-empirical-concentration}). Numerical experiments (Appendix~\ref{sec:experiments}) confirm the depth scaling, conditioning bounds, and proxy--empirical concentration. All condition-number statements refer to the proxy; we do not claim these bounds govern optimization dynamics.
\end{abstract}
