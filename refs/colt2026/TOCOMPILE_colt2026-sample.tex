\begin{abstract}

In the lazy regime, training deep neural networks reduces to kernel regression, and the spectrum of the neural tangent kernel (NTK) controls convergence and stability. Deep networks with low-rank bottlenecks (RF-LR) freeze random feature maps and train only narrow readouts of dimension \(r\ll N\) per layer, achieving 95--99\% fewer parameters than full-width MLPs at comparable depth. We study how depth \(L\) and bottleneck rank \(r\) shape this kernel in the sequential infinite-width limit and as \(r\to\infty\). We derive an explicit NTK recursion for RF-LR with a visible \(1/r\) factor at each bottleneck layer and a closed-form expansion at any depth. For the deterministic (proxy) kernel we prove sharp maximum eigenvalue linear in depth scaling and condition-number bounds: \(\kappa\ge\Omega(r\cdot L)\) in general, and \(\kappa_\perp=1\) or \(1+o(1)\) for high-dimensional spherical data. Under a fixed parameter budget \(O(NLr)\), depth and rank trade off and \emph{commute} from a conditioning perspective: the maximum eigenvalue \(\lambda_{\max}\) of the centered Gram matrix stays bounded, so the stable learning rate \(2/\lambda_{\max}\) does not shrink with depth; one can thus choose deeper RF-LR with the same optimization bounds as shallower ones and retain the approximation benefits of depth. We show that \emph{low rank does not shrink the kernel function class}: the three-layer mean RF-LR kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel (expressivity preserved). We prove a rigorous proxy--empirical bound for equicorrelated data; for general data the concentration is sketched and left open. Numerical experiments confirm depth scaling, conditioning, and proxy--empirical concentration. RKHS equivalence holds for three layers, with \(L\ge 4\) left to future work.
\end{abstract}
