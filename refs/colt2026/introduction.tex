
\section{Introduction}

Low-rank random-feature networks (RF-LR) freeze random feature directions and train only linear readouts (and biases) into a bottleneck of dimension \(r\ll N\) at each layer~\cite{zhang2025structuredbalancedmulticomponentmultilayer}.
In the lazy/NTK regime, training linearizes and reduces to kernel regression with an (approximately) fixed Gram matrix~\cite{jacot2018neural}.
This paper asks: \emph{how do depth \(L\) and bottleneck dimension \(r\) control conditioning-relevant scales for optimization in kernel regression?}
We adopt the standard NTK setup (ReLU activation, isotropic Gaussian initialization, sequential infinite-width limit) and analyze the induced kernel at initialization.

\paragraph{Main results and contributions.}
In the lazy/NTK regime, gradient flow reduces to kernel regression with an (approximately) fixed kernel~\cite{jacot2018neural}. Our main results are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Recursion.} The RF-LR NTK satisfies an explicit infinite-width recursion with an explicit \(1/r\) bottleneck factor, yielding a closed-form expansion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion} and Corollary~\ref{cor:general_L_layer_ntk}).
    \item \textbf{Depth/rank scaling and condition number.} For the deterministic proxy mean recursion, depth drives correlations toward \(1\), the kernel magnitude saturates, and the diagonal--off-diagonal gap decays as \(\asymp 1/(rk)\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}). All condition-number statements refer to the \emph{proxy} Gram matrix. We prove a \emph{lower bound} \(\kappa\ge\Omega(r\cdot L)\) in general (Proposition~\ref{prop:condition-number-depth}). In one special case---equicorrelated data or high-dimensional random data (pairwise correlations \(O(1/\sqrt{d})\) w.h.p.)---we give exact conditioning: \(\kappa_\perp=1\) or \(\kappa_\perp=1+o(1)\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:conditioning-equicorrelated-highdim}). Under a fixed parameter budget, depth and rank trade off from a conditioning perspective.
    \item \textbf{Low rank is enough for the NTK RKHS (three-layer case).} In the three-layer case, the mean RF-LR kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel (Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}), i.e.\ low rank preserves kernel-regime expressivity while making deep kernel models parameter-efficient (\(O(rN)\) trainable parameters per layer rather than \(O(N^2)\)); \(L\ge 4\) remains open.
\end{itemize}
Formal statements: Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}, Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}, Proposition~\ref{prop:condition-number-depth}, Corollary~\ref{cor:conditioning-equicorrelated-highdim}, Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}. Proof sketch: Appendix~\ref{appendix:techniques-proof-sketch}.

\subsection{Architecture}
The architecture stacks high-then-low width layers with a bottleneck \(r\ll n_1\); we train readouts and biases while keeping feature directions frozen.
In the kernel regime, the Gram matrix spectrum controls optimization rates~\cite{jacot2018neural,cao2020understandingspectralbiasdeep}. We characterize limiting kernels and spectra under a composition-of-GPs view in the sequential infinite-width limit.

\subsection{Related work}
The NTK framework~\cite{jacot2018neural} characterizes infinitely wide networks as kernel methods. Extensions cover finite-width corrections~\cite{arora2019exact,dyer2019asymptoticswidenetworksfeynman}, depth scaling~\cite{yang2019scaling,terjek2022depth}, and random-matrix limits for Gram spectra~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent}. Low-rank architectures reduce parameters and change optimization dynamics~\cite{sainath2013low,novikov2015tensorizing,arora2018optimization}; freezing features isolates \(r\) as the key control parameter. LoRA theory situates low-rank fine-tuning between kernel and feature-learning regimes~\cite{dayi2024gradientdynamicslowrankfinetuning,jang2024loratrainingntkregime}. For ReLU kernels, Bietti and Bach~\cite{bietti2021deepequalsshallowrelu} established the ``deep equals shallow'' RKHS phenomenon via endpoint expansions; we combine Fisher--Kibble decoupling~\cite{fisher1915probable,hotelling1953new} with a Puiseux analysis near \(\rho=1\).

\paragraph{Scope, regime, roadmap.}
We study the RF-LR NTK in the sequential infinite-width limit and its deterministic proxy; we do not analyze feature learning, adaptive optimizers, or continual learning. Regime: fix \(n\), take \(N\to\infty\), then study concentration as \(r\to\infty\); we do not pursue \(n\to\infty\) bulk spectral laws here. Sections~\ref{sec:network-definition}--\ref{sec:no-rkhs-advantage} define the architecture, derive the recursion, state depth--rank scaling, and give the RKHS identification.
