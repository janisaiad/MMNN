
\section{Introduction}

Low-rank random-feature networks (RF-LR) are architectures where each layer is implemented as a random feature map followed by a trainable linear readout into a low-dimensional bottleneck~\cite{zhang2025structuredbalancedmulticomponentmultilayer}. Concretely, for each layer we sample frozen random feature directions \(w^{(\ell)}\) and train the readouts \(A^{(\ell)}\) (and biases), which map the feature vector into a bottleneck of dimension \(r\). This reduces the trainable parameter count from \(O(N^2)\) (dense layers) to \(O(rN)\) while keeping the random-feature geometry explicit.

Low-rank parameterizations and factorizations have long been used to reduce memory and computation in neural networks, and to tailor optimization under explicit parameter budgets~\cite{sainath2013low,novikov2015tensorizing,arora2018optimization,kamalakara2022exploringlowranktraining}. In such models one can often separate an upstream representation map from a downstream linear predictor, and the choice of which component is learned can materially affect both optimization and generalization. Recent information-theoretic generalization bounds based on conditional mutual information suggest that stochastic projection/quantization combined with learning a downstream predictor can yield tighter guarantees, providing further motivation for architectures that keep the ``left'' projection map fixed (or randomized) while training the ``right'' readout map~\cite{sefidgaran2025tightercmibasedgeneralizationbounds}.
Recent empirical work by 
 \textit{Zhang et al.}~\cite{zhang2025structuredbalancedmulticomponentmultilayer} has demonstrated that 
 RF-LRs achieve global strong-convergence across a wide range of tasks, including neural-network based PDE solving with and without Physics-Informed losses~\cite{raissi2017physicsinformeddeeplearning}. 
Empirically, training can display stepwise dynamics (plateaus separated by sharp drops) in some regimes~\cite{zhang2025structuredbalancedmulticomponentmultilayer}. This paper does not aim to explain these phenomena beyond the kernel regime; instead we develop an NTK-level theory for RF-LR at initialization and in the lazy/NTK regime, focusing on conditioning/optimization scales and on concentration in the bottleneck dimension \(r\).

The Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework~\cite{jacot2018neural} provides the natural lens 
for analyzing the early training stage. This perspective has been extended to finite width \cite{huang2019dynamicsdeepneuralnetworks,dyer2019asymptoticswidenetworksfeynman} and large-depth \cite{guillen2025finitewidthneuraltangentkernels,yang2023tensorprogramsvifeature,terjek2022depth}, 
revealing how the Laplacian kernel structure governs the gradient descent (GD) training dynamics and generalization. 
Applying NTK theory to RF-LR raises specific technical questions: the kernel recursion differs from the standard MLP recursion because only the readouts (and biases) are trained, and the bottleneck dimension \(r\) induces additional concentration and scaling effects.

\paragraph{Main results (informal).}
\begin{theorem}[Main results in the NTK regime (informal)]\label{thm:main-results-informal}
Consider RF-LR with ReLU features under sequential infinite width \(N\to\infty\) and bottleneck dimension \(r\).
In the lazy/NTK regime, gradient flow reduces to kernel regression with an (approximately) fixed kernel~\cite{jacot2018neural}.
Our main results are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Recursion.} The RF-LR NTK satisfies an explicit infinite-width recursion with an explicit \(1/r\) bottleneck factor, yielding a closed-form expansion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion} and Corollary~\ref{cor:general_L_layer_ntk}).
    \item \textbf{Main: depth--rank scaling and optimization scale.} In an equicorrelated dataset model, the Gram spectrum is explicit and the nontrivial spectrum on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) has scale \(\lambda_\perp=\Theta(1/(rL))\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-spectrum}); for centered targets and \(1/n\)-normalized square loss this yields an optimization time scale \(\Theta(n/\lambda_\perp)=\Theta(nrL)\), which becomes \(\Theta(P/n)\) under the budget scaling \(P\asymp LrN\) and \(N\asymp n^2\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:np-optimization-scale}).
    \item \textbf{RKHS (three layers; secondary consequence).} The mean three-layer kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel (Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

We adopt the standard NTK setup (ReLU activation, isotropic Gaussian initialization, and a sequential infinite-width limit). Accordingly, we analyze the induced kernel at initialization and its deterministic limits.

The loss landscape of neural networks is closely related to generalization and trainability, and in the kernel regime the spectral properties of the Gram matrix control optimization rates and spectral bias~\cite{jacot2018neural,cao2020understandingspectralbiasdeep}. We focus on this kernel-regime viewpoint for RF-LR by characterizing the limiting kernels and their spectra at initialization under explicit assumptions.



Our analysis views intermediate-layer random variables as outputs of a composition of (conditional) Gaussian processes in the sequential infinite-width limit, which enables explicit computations of mean kernels and fluctuations.

\paragraph{Guiding question.}
\begin{center}
\emph{Is low rank all we need, and how does optimization in neural networks interact with the curse of dimensionality?}
\end{center}
This question is closely related to recent discussions of high-dimensional obstacles in neural network optimization~\cite{na2025cursedimensionalityneuralnetwork}. In the NTK regime, the relevant function class is the RKHS induced by the limiting kernel; in particular, low-rank structure does not remove the high-dimensional spectral decay inherent to ReLU kernels on the sphere (Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}).

\paragraph{Progress on the open question.}
Recent work has begun to clarify which aspects of optimization degrade with dimension and which admit dimension-robust descriptions. On the negative side, explicit high-dimensional obstruction results indicate that optimization can become increasingly constrained as the ambient dimension grows, even under benign data models~\cite{na2025cursedimensionalityneuralnetwork}. On the positive side, particle and optimal-transport viewpoints provide regimes where the loss landscape and dynamics admit tractable characterizations, including asymptotic convexity phenomena and dimension-free bounds in two-layer settings~\cite{rotskoff2018neural,mei2018mean,mei2019mean,chizat2018global}. In the kernel regime, recent quantitative bounds on the smallest NTK eigenvalue for deep ReLU networks connect data/width scaling to optimization rates~\cite{nguyen2022tightboundssmallesteigenvalue}. Complementary perspectives based on dynamical invariants and ``central flow'' structure further suggest organizing principles for optimization in deep models beyond the strictly kernel setting~\cite{cohen2025understandingoptimizationdeeplearning}.

\subsection{Related work}
The Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework~\cite{jacot2018neural} characterizes infinitely wide neural networks trained by gradient descent as kernel methods with a fixed kernel. Extensions cover finite-width corrections~\cite{arora2019exact,dyer2019asymptoticswidenetworksfeynman}, depth scaling~\cite{yang2019scaling,terjek2022depth}, and random-matrix limits for Gram spectra in quadratic regimes~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent}.

Low-rank and factorized architectures reduce trainable parameters and can change optimization dynamics~\cite{sainath2013low,novikov2015tensorizing,arora2018optimization}. In RF-LR/MMNN, freezing the feature directions and training only readouts (and biases) isolates the bottleneck dimension \(r\) as a natural control parameter for kernel concentration and depth scaling.

From the standpoint of generalization, stochastic projections and compression mechanisms can be beneficial when combined with learning a final predictor; see, e.g., the conditional mutual information analysis of~\cite{sefidgaran2025tightercmibasedgeneralizationbounds}. This supports studying regimes where upstream random feature maps are kept fixed and the downstream readout is trained.

Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is a widely used parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for foundation models, where training is restricted to a low-rank update of a pre-trained weight matrix. Recent theory begins to locate LoRA between the kernel/lazy regime and full feature learning: in a student--teacher setting,~\cite{dayi2024gradientdynamicslowrankfinetuning} shows that low-rank fine-tuning can exhibit nonlinear dynamics beyond linearized kernels while remaining efficiently learnable under online gradient descent. Complementarily, in the NTK regime,~\cite{jang2024loratrainingntkregime} establishes that sufficiently large LoRA rank can eliminate spurious local minima and enables gradient descent to find low-rank solutions. These results reinforce the broader motivation for understanding which parts of a factorized parameterization should be trained and how low-rank constraints shape optimization and generalization.

For rotation-invariant ReLU kernels, the ``deep equals shallow'' phenomenon and RKHS characterization via endpoint expansions were established in~\cite{bietti2021deepequalsshallowrelu}. We leverage this framework in the RF-LR setting by combining Fisher--Kibble decoupling~\cite{fisher1915probable,hotelling1953new} with a hypergeometric/Puiseux analysis of the mean kernel near \(\rho=1\).


\subsection{Our contributions}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-results-informal} summarizes our main contributions; the formal statements are Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}, Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}, Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-spectrum}, Corollary~\ref{cor:np-optimization-scale}, and Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}.
Our main techniques and proof sketch are summarized in Appendix~\ref{appendix:techniques-proof-sketch}.

\paragraph{Scope and guarantees (what we analyze and what we do not claim).}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objects analyzed.} We study (i) the RF-LR NTK recursion in the sequential infinite-width limit (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}); (ii) an exact depth--rank scaling theorem for the Gram spectrum in an equicorrelated dataset model (Section~\ref{sec:depth-scaling}); and (iii) rank-driven concentration and mean-kernel consequences in the three-layer case (Section~\ref{sec:no-rkhs-advantage} and Appendix~\ref{appendix:techniques-proof-sketch}).
    \item \textbf{Optimization in-scope.} In the lazy/NTK regime, gradient flow reduces to kernel regression with an (approximately) fixed kernel (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}). Convergence rates are governed by the training-set Gram spectrum; our contribution is to give explicit depth--rank scaling predictions for this spectrum in RF-LR.
    \item \textbf{Not claimed.} We do not analyze feature learning (time evolution of the NTK), adaptive optimizers, or continual-learning/forgetting phenomena. Any references to such effects are purely motivational and are not used as claims or evidence.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Regimes.}
We work in a finite-sample kernel regime (\emph{Regime 1}): we fix a dataset of size \(n\), take a sequential infinite-width limit \(N\to\infty\), and then study how the remaining bottleneck randomness concentrates as \(r\to\infty\).
We \emph{do not} pursue the quadratic random-matrix regime \(n\to\infty\) and bulk spectral laws for the \emph{empirical} RF-LR NTK here (\emph{Regime 2}). Bulk laws in this scaling have recently been established for (shallow) two-layer NTKs via resolvent replacement and Gaussian-equivalence methods~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent}; extending such techniques to the RF-LR low-rank setting and to depth \(L\ge 2\) is left for future work.
