\section{Depth scaling of the RF-LR NTK}\label{sec:depth-scaling}

We adapt the depth-scaling viewpoint of~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc} (inverse-cosine-distance propagation and its spectral consequences) to our doubly random RF-LR kernel. A crucial caveat is that the explicit closed form for the \emph{MLP} NTK at EOC does \emph{not} directly apply to RF-LR: RF-LR trains only the low-rank readouts \(A^{(\ell)}\) with frozen random features \(w^{(\ell)}\), hence the NTK is governed by our recursion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}) rather than the full-MLP formula. What does carry over is the geometry of \emph{activation correlations} and the associated inverse cosine distance propagation, which we use to control \(\Sigma^{(\ell)}\) and \(\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}\) across depth.

\subsection{Correlation propagation and inverse cosine distances}

For ReLU activation, the RF-LR architecture uses the same EOC parameterization as standard MLPs~\cite{hayou2018selectioninitializationactivationfunction}. The forward correlation map induced by ReLU is the same as in~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}; in particular, if we define a deterministic cosine recursion \( \rho_k = \varrho(\rho_{k-1}) \) (the infinite-width/full-rank idealization), then for ReLU \( \Delta_\phi = 1/2 \) (i.e. \( a = b = 1/\sqrt{2} \) in \( (a,b) \)-ReLU notation).

\begin{definition}[Cosine map for ReLU at EOC]
For ReLU, set \( \Delta_\phi = 1/2 \). The cosine map \( \varrho : [-1,1] \to [-1,1] \) and its derivative \( \varrho' \) satisfy
\[
\varrho(\rho) = \rho + \Delta_\phi\,\frac{2}{\pi}\bigl( \sqrt{1-\rho^2} - \rho\arccos(\rho) \bigr),\qquad
\varrho'(\rho) = 1 - \Delta_\phi\,\frac{2}{\pi}\arccos(\rho).
\]
We write \( \rho_k = \varrho(\rho_{k-1}) \) for the (deterministic) limiting correlation at layer \( k \).
\end{definition}

To a cosine \( \rho \in (-1,1) \), we associate the squared cosine distance \( z = (1-\rho)/2 \in (0,1) \) and the inverse cosine distance \( w = z^{-1/2} \in (1,\infty) \).

\begin{definition}[Squared and inverse cosine distance maps]
Define \( \zeta : [0,1] \to [0,1] \) by \( \zeta(z) = (1 - \varrho(1-2z))/2 \) and \( \omega : (1,\infty) \to (1,\infty) \) by \( \omega(w) = \zeta(w^{-2})^{-1/2} \). Then \( \omega \) is convex and satisfies
\[
\omega(w) = w + \Delta_\phi\,\frac{4}{3\pi} + \frac{3}{2}\Bigl(\Delta_\phi\,\frac{4}{3\pi}\Bigr)^2 w^{-1} + O(w^{-2}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse cosine distance propagation for RF-LR]\label{prop:rflr-inverse-cosine-prop}
Given \( w \in (1,\infty) \), for \( k \in \mathbb{N} \) we have
\[
\bigl\vert \omega^{\circ k}(w) - \bigl( w + \Delta_\phi\,\tfrac{4}{3\pi}(k-1) + \Delta_\phi\,\tfrac{2}{\pi}\log\bigl( \Delta_\phi^{-1}\,\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\,w + k - 1 \bigr) \bigr) \bigr\vert \le O(1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is a statement about the iterates of the inverse cosine distance map \(\omega\), so what we must justify is that \(\omega\) is indeed the correct map governing the \emph{RF-LR} correlation recursion.

\paragraph{Step 1: RF-LR correlation map (idealized) equals the ReLU cosine map.}
Fix a layer \(\ell\ge 2\) and two inputs \((x,x')\). Write the RF-LR layer as
\[
\bm{h}^{(\ell)}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_\ell} A^{(\ell)}_{j}\,\ReLU\!\big(w^{(\ell)\top}_j\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x)\big),
\qquad
\bm{h}^{(\ell)}(x') = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_\ell} A^{(\ell)}_{j}\,\ReLU\!\big(w^{(\ell)\top}_j\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x')\big),
\]
where \(A^{(\ell)}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\) is the (random) readout column and \(w^{(\ell)}_j\) is the frozen random feature direction. Assume the standard RF-LR initialization: \(\{A^{(\ell)}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_\ell}\) are i.i.d. centered with \(\mathbb{E}[A^{(\ell)}_{j}A^{(\ell)\top}_{j}]=\sigma_A^2 I_r\), independent of \(\{w^{(\ell)}_j\}_{j=1}^{n_\ell}\), and take the sequential infinite-width limit \(n_\ell\to\infty\) (Definition~\ref{def:base-derivative-kernels}).

Condition on \(\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x),\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x')\). By independence across \(j\),
\[
\mathbb{E}\big[\bm{h}^{(\ell)}(x)\cdot \bm{h}^{(\ell)}(x') \,\big|\, \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}\big]
= \frac{1}{n_\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{n_\ell}\mathbb{E}\big[\|A^{(\ell)}_{j}\|^2\big]\,
\mathbb{E}\big[\ReLU(U_j)\ReLU(V_j)\,\big|\,\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}\big],
\]
where \((U_j,V_j)=(w_j^{(\ell)\top}\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x),\,w_j^{(\ell)\top}\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}(x'))\) is a centered bivariate Gaussian conditional on \(\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}\), with correlation equal to the (random) sample cosine \(\rho_{\ell-1}(x,x')\). The same computation gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bm{h}^{(\ell)}(x)\|^2 \,\big|\, \bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}\big]
= \frac{1}{n_\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{n_\ell}\mathbb{E}\big[\|A^{(\ell)}_{j}\|^2\big]\,
\mathbb{E}\big[\ReLU(U_j)^2\,\big|\,\bm{h}^{(\ell-1)}\big],
\]
and similarly for \(x'\). Passing to the limit \(n_\ell\to\infty\), the empirical averages converge to the corresponding ReLU Gaussian expectations; crucially, the (isotropic) factor \(\mathbb{E}\|A^{(\ell)}_{j}\|^2\) cancels in the cosine ratio.
Therefore, in the \emph{deterministic mean-field idealization} (where one replaces the random sample cosines \(\rho_{\ell-1}(x,x')\) by their deterministic correlation coefficients and studies the induced map on those coefficients), the RF-LR correlation recursion is governed by the same ReLU cosine map \(\varrho\) as in~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}.
For finite bottleneck dimension \(r\), \(\rho_{\ell-1}(x,x')\) remains random; justifying the deterministic recursion requires \(r\)-dependent concentration (which in this work is handled via Fisher--Kibble/Fisher-type asymptotics and leads to \(O(r^{-1/2})\)-scale fluctuations).

\paragraph{Step 2: reduce to iterates of \(\omega\).}
With \(z_k=(1-\rho_k)/2\) and \(w_k=z_k^{-1/2}\), we have \(z_{k}=\zeta(z_{k-1})\) and therefore \(w_k=\omega(w_{k-1})\), i.e. \(w_k=\omega^{\circ(k-1)}(w_1)\).

\paragraph{Step 3: asymptotics of \(\omega^{\circ k}\).}
Since \(\omega\) is exactly the same inverse cosine distance map as in~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc} (it is defined from the ReLU cosine map \(\varrho\)), the claimed bound is precisely the iterate asymptotic proved in~\cite[Proposition~3]{terjek2025mlpsateoc}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{RF-LR NTK: recursion-driven depth scaling (not the full-MLP closed form)}

For full-width MLPs at EOC, the limiting NTK admits an explicit expression in terms of the cosine map and its derivative~\cite[Proposition~4]{terjek2025mlpsateoc}. This identity relies on the fact that \emph{all} weight layers are trained. In contrast, RF-LR trains only the low-rank readouts \(A^{(\ell)}\) and freezes \(w^{(\ell)}\); therefore the RF-LR NTK is given by our recursion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}) and its closed form (Corollary~\ref{cor:general_L_layer_ntk}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ntk_explicit_form}) in terms of \(\Sigma^{(\ell)}\) and \(\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}\).

The Terjék machinery is still useful here, but at the level of controlling the depth-evolution of correlations (hence of \(\Sigma^{(\ell)}\) and \(\dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}\)), not by importing the full-MLP NTK identity.

\begin{proposition}[Geometric suppression of early layers under bottlenecks]\label{prop:rflr-depth-geometric}
Assume \(r>1\), and for all \(\ell\ge 2\) and all input pairs \((x,x')\) we have the bounds \(0\le \dot{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}(x,x')\le 1\) and \(|\Sigma^{(\ell)}(x,x')|\le C\,\|x\|\,\|x'\|\) for some constant \(C>0\). Then the RF-LR NTK entries satisfy the uniform depth bound
\[
\bigl|\Kop^{(L)}(x,x')\bigr|
\;\le\;
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \frac{1}{r^{L-\ell}}
\;+\;
C\,\|x\|\,\|x'\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{1}{r^{\max(1,L-\ell)}}
\;\le\;
\frac{r}{r-1} \;+\; \frac{C\,\|x\|\,\|x'\|}{r-1},
\]
and each layer-\(\ell\) contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ntk_explicit_form} is suppressed by the factor \(r^{-(L-\ell)}\) times the product of subsequent \(\dot{\Sigma}^{(k)}\) terms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Main qualitative difference to full-width EOC NTK]
In~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}, the full-width MLP EOC NTK has \(\lambda_{\max}\) growing on the order of \(L\). Proposition~\ref{prop:rflr-depth-geometric} shows that, in RF-LR, the explicit \(1/r\) bottleneck scaling forces a \emph{geometric} depth suppression: for fixed \(r>1\), the RF-LR NTK entries (hence operator norms) do not grow linearly with depth.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Inverse cosine distance matrices and spectral bounds}

To formulate dataset-level depth scaling, we work with the deterministic correlation recursion (the mean/infinite-width idealization). Given a dataset \( \{x_1,\ldots,x_n\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d} \), set
\[
\rho_{1,ij} \;=\; \frac{\langle x_i,x_j\rangle}{\|x_i\|\,\|x_j\|}\in[-1,1],
\qquad
\rho_{k,ij} \;=\; \varrho(\rho_{k-1,ij}),\quad k\ge 2.
\]
Define the layer-\( k \) inverse cosine distance matrix \( W_k \in \mathbb{S}^n \) by
\[
(W_k)_{i,i} = 0,\qquad (W_k)_{i_1,i_2} = \biggl( \frac{1 - \rho_{k,i_1 i_2}}{2} \biggr)^{-1/2},\quad i_1 \neq i_2,
\]
so that \(W_k\) is the inverse cosine distance matrix associated to \(\rho_k\).

\begin{proposition}[Spectral bounds for RF-LR inverse cosine distance matrices]\label{prop:rflr-w-spectral}
Assume EOC initialization and a dataset with no parallel pairs. For each \( k \in [1:L] \) there exists \( \widehat{W}_k \in (1,\infty) \) with \( \widehat{W}_k = \Theta_{W_1}(1) \) such that
\[
\bigl\Vert W_k - \omega^{\circ(k-1)}(\widehat{W}_k)\,(\mathbf{1}_n\mathbf{1}_n^\top - I_n) \bigr\Vert \le O(\Delta_\phi\, n^{-1} k) + O(1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is exactly~\cite[Proposition~6]{terjek2025mlpsateoc} applied to the deterministic recursion \( \rho_{k,ij}=\varrho(\rho_{k-1,ij}) \). The proof uses only convexity/monotonicity of \( \omega \) and matrix comparison arguments.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral consequences (depth scaling)}

Proposition~\ref{prop:rflr-depth-geometric} implies that, for fixed \(r>1\), the depth-dependence of the RF-LR NTK is governed by a geometric series rather than a linear-in-\(L\) growth. Consequently, any operator-norm bound for the RF-LR Gram matrix \( \mathbf{K}^{(L)} \) obtained from entrywise control and standard matrix inequalities will be \emph{uniform} in \(L\), in sharp contrast with the full-width EOC NTK of~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}.

Deriving Terjék-tight eigenvalue and condition-number bounds for \( \mathbf{K}^{(L)} \) in RF-LR requires combining: (i)~the inverse-cosine-distance control of correlations (Propositions~\ref{prop:rflr-inverse-cosine-prop} and~\ref{prop:rflr-w-spectral}); with (ii)~the RF-LR NTK expansion \eqref{eq:ntk_explicit_form} that introduces the \(1/r\) bottleneck weights.

\subsection{A Terjék-style depth theorem for the RF-LR NTK (new kernel, new scaling)}\label{subsec:rflr-terjek-generalization}

We now give an explicit depth scaling statement for the \emph{mean} RF-LR NTK that is not a copy of~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}: the RF-LR recursion contains an explicit \(1/r\) bottleneck factor, so the kernel magnitude \emph{saturates} in depth, while the diagonal--off-diagonal gap decays with depth in the mean recursion.

\paragraph{Scalar ReLU kernels as functions of cosine similarity.}
For unit-variance ReLU features, write \( \theta=\arccos(\rho) \). The normalized base and derivative kernels are
\[
s(\rho) \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl((\pi-\theta)\cos\theta + \sin\theta\bigr),
\qquad
\dot{s}(\rho) \;=\; \frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Near \( \rho=1 \), parameterize by the inverse cosine distance \( w=((1-\rho)/2)^{-1/2} \), so that \( \rho = 1-2w^{-2} \) and \( \theta = 2w^{-1}+O(w^{-3}) \). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relu-kernel-expansions-w}
\dot{s}(\rho) \;=\; \frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi w}+O(w^{-3}),
\qquad
s(\rho)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{w^{2}}+O(w^{-3}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Deterministic proxy (mean-field) RF-LR entrywise recursion.}
Fix an input pair \((x,x')\) with input cosine similarity \(\rho_1\in(-1,1)\). Define a \emph{deterministic} correlation recursion \(\rho_{k}=\varrho(\rho_{k-1})\) and \(w_k=((1-\rho_k)/2)^{-1/2}\). Define the scalar deterministic proxy RF-LR NTK by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rflr-scalar-mean-ntk-rec}
\Theta^{(1)}(\rho_1) \;=\; 1 + s(\rho_1),
\qquad
\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1) \;=\; 1 + \frac{1}{r}\,\Theta^{(k-1)}(\rho_1)\,\dot{s}(\rho_k) + \frac{1}{r}\,s(\rho_k),\quad k\ge 2.
\end{equation}
This recursion should be viewed as a \emph{mean-field proxy}: it is obtained from Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion} by replacing the random fields \(\Sigma^{(k)},\dot{\Sigma}^{(k)}\) with their scalar ReLU dual functions \(s(\cdot),\dot{s}(\cdot)\) evaluated along the deterministic correlation coefficients \(\rho_k\).
Importantly, for finite bottleneck dimension \(r\), the \emph{sample} cosine similarity
\(\langle \bm{h}^{(k)}(x),\bm{h}^{(k)}(x')\rangle/(\|\bm{h}^{(k)}(x)\|\,\|\bm{h}^{(k)}(x')\|)\)
is random; (11) does not assert that this random cosine equals \(\rho_k\) almost surely, nor that \(\Theta^{(k)}\) equals the exact annealed mean \(\mathbb{E}[\Kop^{(k)}(x,x')]\) of the full doubly-random deep GP.
Rather, \(\rho_k\) is the deterministic correlation \emph{parameter} governing the covariance of the limiting (conditional) Gaussian pair at depth \(k\), and the usefulness of (11) rests on \(r\)-concentration: for a jointly Gaussian pair in \(\mathbb{R}^r\) with correlation parameter \(\rho_k\), the induced sample cosine concentrates around \(\rho_k\) with variance \(O(1/r)\) (equivalently, \(\mathbb{E}|\,\widehat{\rho}_k-\rho_k\,|\lesssim r^{-1/2}\)).

\begin{theorem}[Depth scaling for the mean RF-LR NTK]\label{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}
Fix \(r>1\) and \(\rho_1\in(-1,1)\). Let \(\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)\) be defined by~\eqref{eq:rflr-scalar-mean-ntk-rec}. Then:
\begin{itemize}
  \item (\textbf{Correlation alignment}) There exist \(k_0\in\mathbb{N}\) and constants \(0<c_-<c_+<\infty\) (depending only on \(\rho_1\)) such that for all \(k\ge k_0\),
  \[
  c_-\,k \;\le\; w_k \;\le\; c_+\,k,
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  1-\rho_k \;=\; 2 w_k^{-2} \;=\; O(k^{-2}).
  \]
  \item (\textbf{Kernel saturation}) \(\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)\to \Theta_\star(r)\) as \(k\to\infty\), where \(\Theta_\star(r)\) is the fixed point of the limiting recursion obtained by setting \(\dot{s}=s=1/2\), namely
  \[
  \Theta_\star(r) \;=\; \frac{2r+1}{2r-1}.
  \]
  Moreover, there exists \(C_\Theta<\infty\) (depending on \(r,\rho_1\)) such that for all \(k\ge 1\),
  \[
  \bigl|\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)-\Theta_\star(r)\bigr| \;\le\; \frac{C_\Theta}{k}.
  \]
  \item (\textbf{Depth-induced gap decay}) Let \(\Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{diag}}=\Theta^{(k)}(1)\) and \(\Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{off}}=\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)\). Then
  \[
  \Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{diag}}-\Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{off}} \;=\; \Theta\!\Big(\frac{1}{r\,k}\Big),
  \]
  i.e. there exist \(k_1\in\mathbb{N}\) and constants \(0<c_\Delta<C_\Delta<\infty\) (depending on \(r,\rho_1\)) such that for all \(k\ge k_1\),
  \[
  \frac{c_\Delta}{r\,k} \;\le\; \Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{diag}}-\Theta^{(k)}_{\mathrm{off}} \;\le\; \frac{C_\Delta}{r\,k}.
  \]
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\paragraph{Step 1: correlation alignment.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:rflr-inverse-cosine-prop}, there exists a constant \(C_w<\infty\) such that for all \(k\ge 1\),
\[
w_k=\omega^{\circ(k-1)}(w_1)
= w_1 + c_0 (k-1) + c_1 \log\!\bigl(c_2 w_1 + k - 1\bigr) + \varepsilon_k,
\qquad |\varepsilon_k|\le C_w,
\]
where \(c_0=\Delta_\phi\,\frac{4}{3\pi}>0\), \(c_1=\Delta_\phi\,\frac{2}{\pi}\), \(c_2=\Delta_\phi^{-1}\frac{3\pi}{4}\). In particular, \(w_k\ge w_1+c_0(k-1)-C_w\), so choosing \(k_0\) large enough gives \(w_k\ge \frac{c_0}{2}k\) for all \(k\ge k_0\). Similarly \(w_k\le c_+k\) for some \(c_+<\infty\) and all \(k\ge k_0\). Thus \(c_-k\le w_k\le c_+k\) for \(k\ge k_0\), and \(1-\rho_k=2w_k^{-2}=O(k^{-2})\).

\paragraph{Step 2: kernel saturation.}
Write \(\Theta_k=\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)\) and rewrite \eqref{eq:rflr-scalar-mean-ntk-rec} as
\[
\Theta_k=b_k+a_k\Theta_{k-1},\qquad a_k=\frac{1}{r}\dot{s}(\rho_k),\qquad b_k=1+\frac{1}{r}s(\rho_k).
\]
For the limiting constant-coefficient recursion (corresponding to \(\rho_k\to 1\)), we have \(a=\frac{1}{r}\dot{s}(1)=\frac{1}{2r}\), \(b=1+\frac{1}{r}s(1)=1+\frac{1}{2r}\), hence \(\Theta_\star(r)=b/(1-a)=(2r+1)/(2r-1)\).

Set \(e_k=\Theta_k-\Theta_\star(r)\). Then
\[
e_k=a_k e_{k-1} + (b_k-b) + (a_k-a)\Theta_\star(r).
\]
Using \eqref{eq:relu-kernel-expansions-w} and \(w_k\ge c_-k\) for \(k\ge k_0\), there exists \(C<\infty\) such that for all \(k\ge k_0\),
\[
|a_k-a|
=\frac{1}{r}\Bigl|\dot{s}(\rho_k)-\frac12\Bigr|
\le \frac{C}{r}\frac{1}{w_k}
\le \frac{C}{r}\frac{1}{k},
\qquad
|b_k-b|
=\frac{1}{r}\Bigl|s(\rho_k)-\frac12\Bigr|
\le \frac{C}{r}\frac{1}{w_k^2}
\le \frac{C}{r}\frac{1}{k^2}.
\]
Moreover \(0\le a_k\le a=1/(2r)<1\). Hence for all \(k\ge k_0\),
\[
|e_k|\le a\,|e_{k-1}|+\frac{C'}{k}
\]
for some \(C'<\infty\). A standard induction (discrete Grönwall) yields \(|e_k|\le C_\Theta/k\) for all \(k\ge 1\), in particular \(e_k\to 0\) and \(\Theta_k\to\Theta_\star(r)\).

To make the \(O(1/k)\) rate explicit, fix \(k\ge k_0\) and iterate the one-step inequality:
\[
|e_k|
\le a^{k-k_0}|e_{k_0}| + \sum_{j=k_0+1}^{k} a^{k-j}\frac{C'}{j}.
\]
Since \(j\ge k_0+1\) implies \(1/j\le 1/k_0\) and also \(j\le k\) implies \(1/j\le 1/k\), we can bound
\[
\sum_{j=k_0+1}^{k} a^{k-j}\frac{1}{j}
\le \frac{1}{k}\sum_{t=0}^{k-k_0-1} a^{t}
\le \frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{1-a}.
\]
Also \(a^{k-k_0}|e_{k_0}|\le |e_{k_0}|\le (k_0|e_{k_0}|)/k\). Therefore for all \(k\ge k_0\),
\[
|e_k|\le \frac{1}{k}\Bigl(k_0|e_{k_0}|+\frac{C'}{1-a}\Bigr).
\]
Absorbing the finitely many values \(k<k_0\) into the constant gives \(|e_k|\le C_\Theta/k\) for all \(k\ge 1\), as claimed.

\paragraph{Step 3: diagonal--off-diagonal gap.}
Let \(\Theta^{\mathrm{diag}}_k=\Theta^{(k)}(1)\) and \(\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_k=\Theta^{(k)}(\rho_1)\), and set \(\Delta_k=\Theta^{\mathrm{diag}}_k-\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_k\ge 0\). The diagonal recursion is \(\Theta^{\mathrm{diag}}_k=b+a\,\Theta^{\mathrm{diag}}_{k-1}\), while the off-diagonal recursion is \(\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_k=b_k+a_k\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_{k-1}\). Subtracting gives the exact identity
\[
\Delta_k
=a\,\Delta_{k-1} + (a-a_k)\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_{k-1} + (b-b_k).
\]
For \(k\) large, \(\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_{k-1}\) is bounded above and below by positive constants (since \(\Theta^{\mathrm{off}}_k\to\Theta_\star(r)\in(1,\infty)\)). Also by \eqref{eq:relu-kernel-expansions-w} and \(w_k\asymp k\),
\[
a-a_k=\frac{1}{r}\Bigl(\frac12-\dot{s}(\rho_k)\Bigr)=\Theta\!\Big(\frac{1}{r\,w_k}\Big)=\Theta\!\Big(\frac{1}{r\,k}\Big),
\qquad
b-b_k=\frac{1}{r}\Bigl(\frac12-s(\rho_k)\Bigr)=O\!\Big(\frac{1}{r\,w_k^2}\Big)=O\!\Big(\frac{1}{r\,k^2}\Big).
\]
Thus there exist \(k_1\) and constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that for all \(k\ge k_1\),
\[
a\,\Delta_{k-1}+\frac{c}{r\,k}\le \Delta_k\le a\,\Delta_{k-1}+\frac{C}{r\,k}.
\]
Iterating these comparison recursions yields \(c_\Delta/(rk)\le \Delta_k\le C_\Delta/(rk)\) for all \(k\ge k_1\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Centered eigenvalue in an equicorrelated model]\label{prop:equicorrelated-gap}
Fix \(L\ge 2\) and \(r>1\). Consider a dataset \(\{x_i\}_{i=1}^n\) with \(\|x_i\|=1\) and \(\rho_{1,ij}=\rho_0\in(-1,1)\) for all \(i\neq j\). Let
\[
K^{(L)}_{ij} := \Theta^{(L)}(\rho_{1,ij}),
\qquad
H := I_n-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top,
\qquad
K^{(L)}_c := HK^{(L)}H.
\]
Then \(K^{(L)}\) has the explicit form \(K^{(L)} = \Theta^{(L)}(1)\,I_n + \Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0)\,(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top-I_n)\). In particular, the nonzero eigenvalues of \(K^{(L)}_c\) are all equal to
\[
\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)}) \;=\; \Theta^{(L)}(1)-\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0),
\]
with multiplicity \(n-1\). Moreover, by Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}, there exist constants \(0<c_\Delta<C_\Delta<\infty\) (depending on \(r,\rho_0\)) such that for all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
\frac{c_\Delta}{r\,L} \;\le\; \lambda_\perp(K^{(L)}) \;\le\; \frac{C_\Delta}{r\,L}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The explicit form of \(K^{(L)}\) follows from \(\rho_{1,ii}=1\) and \(\rho_{1,ij}=\rho_0\) for \(i\neq j\). The matrix \(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top-I_n\) has eigenvalue \(n-1\) along \(\mathbf{1}\) and eigenvalue \(-1\) on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\). Therefore \(K^{(L)}\) has eigenvalue \(\Theta^{(L)}(1)+(n-1)\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0)\) along \(\mathbf{1}\) and eigenvalue \(\Theta^{(L)}(1)-\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0)\) on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\). Since \(H\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\), the nonzero eigenvalues of \(K^{(L)}_c=HK^{(L)}H\) are exactly the \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) eigenvalues of \(K^{(L)}\), with multiplicity \(n-1\). The \(1/(rL)\) bounds then follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean} applied to \(\rho_1=\rho_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uncentered spike and bulk scaling in the equicorrelated model]\label{prop:equicorrelated-spectrum}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap}, the eigenvalues of the \emph{uncentered} Gram matrix \(K^{(L)}\) are explicit:
\[
\lambda_1\!\bigl(K^{(L)}\bigr) \;=\; \Theta^{(L)}(1) + (n-1)\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0),
\qquad
\lambda_2\!\bigl(K^{(L)}\bigr)=\cdots=\lambda_n\!\bigl(K^{(L)}\bigr) \;=\; \Theta^{(L)}(1)-\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0).
\]
In particular, \(\lambda_1(K^{(L)})\) is a rank-one ``spike'' aligned with \(\mathbf{1}\), while the spectrum on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) equals \(\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})\).

Moreover, as \(L\to\infty\) (with \(r\) fixed) the spike \emph{saturates}:
\[
\lambda_1\!\bigl(K^{(L)}\bigr)
\;=\;
n\,\Theta_\star(r) \;+\; O\!\Big(\frac{n}{L}\Big),
\qquad
\Theta_\star(r)=\frac{2r+1}{2r-1}=1+O\!\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big),
\]
and the \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) eigenvalue satisfies \(\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})=\Theta(1/(rL))\) by Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap}. Thus, in this model the depth/rank scaling is:
\[
\lambda_{\max}(K^{(L)}) \asymp n\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr),\qquad
\lambda_{\min}\!\bigl(HK^{(L)}H\bigr) \asymp \frac{1}{rL}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The eigenvalue identities are the \(\mathbf{1}\) and \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) eigenvalues established in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap}. The spike saturation follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}: both \(\Theta^{(L)}(1)\) and \(\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0)\) converge to \(\Theta_\star(r)\) at rate \(O(1/L)\), hence
\[
\bigl|\lambda_1(K^{(L)})-n\Theta_\star(r)\bigr|
\le \bigl|\Theta^{(L)}(1)-\Theta_\star(r)\bigr| + (n-1)\bigl|\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_0)-\Theta_\star(r)\bigr|
= O\!\Big(\frac{n}{L}\Big).
\]
Finally, \(\lambda_{\max}(K^{(L)})\asymp n(1+1/r)\) follows from \(\lambda_1(K^{(L)})\sim n\Theta_\star(r)\) and \(\Theta_\star(r)=(2r+1)/(2r-1)=1+O(1/r)\), while \(\lambda_{\min}(HK^{(L)}H)=\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})\asymp 1/(rL)\) follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conditioning: uncentered vs centered]\label{rmk:conditioning-centered}
The scaling \(\lambda_{\max}(K^{(L)})\asymp n(1+1/r)\) together with \(\lambda_{\min}(HK^{(L)}H)\asymp 1/(rL)\) should \emph{not} be read as a statement that ``the condition number worsens like \(nrL\)'': these quantities live on different invariant subspaces.
For mean-zero targets (or after centering), the relevant operator is \(HK^{(L)}H\) on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\).
In the equicorrelated model, \(HK^{(L)}H\) has a single nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})\) with multiplicity \(n-1\), hence its condition number on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) equals \(1\) for every \(L,r\).
What does deteriorate with \(L\) and \(r\) here is the \emph{scale} \(\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})\), which sets the natural time/step-size scaling in kernel gradient descent (one can compensate by rescaling the learning rate in the idealized NTK dynamics).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Can the smallest eigenvalue become negative?]\label{rmk:lambda-min-negative}
No: for any finite network, the empirical NTK Gram matrix \(\widehat{K}\) at initialization is a Gram matrix of gradients,
\(\widehat{K}_{ij}=\langle \nabla_\theta f(x_i),\nabla_\theta f(x_j)\rangle\), hence \(\widehat{K}\succeq 0\) deterministically and all its eigenvalues are \(\ge 0\).
Moreover, centering preserves positive semidefiniteness: \(H\widehat{K}H\succeq 0\) since \(H\) is an orthogonal projection.

What \emph{can} happen is that the deterministic proxy lower bound (e.g. \(\lambda_{\min}(HK^{(L)}H)\asymp 1/(rL)\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap}) is smaller than the magnitude of random fluctuations.
In general, Weyl's inequality gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}(H\widehat{K}H)\;\ge\;\lambda_{\min}(HKH)\;-\;\|H(\widehat{K}-K)H\|,
\]
so if \(\|H(\widehat{K}-K)H\|\) is larger than \(1/(rL)\), this bound becomes vacuous (it may be negative), but the true eigenvalue still satisfies \(\lambda_{\min}(H\widehat{K}H)\ge 0\).
Thus, the relevant concern is not ``going below zero'' but rather ``being driven close to zero'' when the \(1/(rL)\) signal is swamped by noise.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[General datasets: heuristic vs theorem]\label{rmk:rank-depth-tradeoff}
Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap} is a theorem under an explicit (equicorrelated) model. For general datasets with heterogeneous \(\rho_{1,ij}\) and non-constant norms, translating the diagonal--off-diagonal gap of the scalar recursion into a uniform lower bound on \(\lambda_{\min}(HKH)\) requires additional explicit assumptions (e.g. norm regularity and coherence bounds on \(\rho_{1,ij}\)). Absent such assumptions, statements of the form ``\(\lambda_{\min}(HKH)\asymp 1/(rL)\)'' should be treated as heuristic rather than as part of the main theorem chain.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Infinite-depth limit: existence and degeneracy after centering]\label{rmk:infinite-depth-limit}
The correlation recursion satisfies \(1-\rho_k=O(k^{-2})\) as in full-width MLPs at the EOC, so deep features become increasingly aligned.
For the \emph{mean} RF-LR NTK recursion \eqref{eq:rflr-scalar-mean-ntk-rec}, Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean} implies that for any fixed \(\rho_1\in(-1,1)\),
\[
\Theta^{(L)}(\rho_1)\to \Theta_\star(r)\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Theta^{(L)}(1)\to \Theta_\star(r),
\]
so the entrywise infinite-depth limit of the (mean) Gram matrix is a constant kernel: \(K^{(L)}\to \Theta_\star(r)\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\).
Consequently, after centering one has \(HK^{(L)}H\to 0\), and the relevant spectrum on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) collapses.
In particular, in the equicorrelated model Proposition~\ref{prop:equicorrelated-gap} gives \(\lambda_{\min}(HK^{(L)}H)=\Theta(1/(rL))\), so a \emph{non-degenerate} deep limit on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) can only hold under a joint scaling (e.g. \(r\) growing with \(L\)) or after an explicit rescaling, such as considering \(rL\,HK^{(L)}H\), whose nonzero eigenvalues are \(rL\,\lambda_\perp(K^{(L)})\in[c_\Delta,C_\Delta]\) for all large \(L\).
\end{remark}
