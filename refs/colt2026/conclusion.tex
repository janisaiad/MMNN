\section{Conclusion}

This work develops an NTK analysis for low-rank random-feature architectures (RF-LR/MMNN) under explicit assumptions. 
\textbf{Expressivity (RKHS; secondary consequence).} In the NTK regime, the three-layer RF-LR mean kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel (Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}), so depth does not enlarge the RKHS in this setting.
\textbf{Concentration in the bottleneck dimension.} For the three-layer case, the empirical kernel concentrates around its deterministic limit \(K_\infty(\rho)\) with tails controlled by the bottleneck dimension \(r\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs_rank_concentration}, Corollary~\ref{cor:three_layer_ntk_concentration}).
\textbf{Centered conditioning and optimization scales.} In an equicorrelated model, the centered Gram matrix \(HKH\) has flat spectrum on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) (condition number \(1\)), and its nontrivial eigenvalue scale is \(\lambda_\perp=\Theta(1/(rL))\) (Section~\ref{sec:depth-scaling}), yielding the budget-driven kernel time scale \(\Theta(P/n)\) under \(P\asymp LrN\) and \(N\asymp n^2\).
\textbf{Recursion.} We provide an explicit NTK recursion for arbitrary depth in RF-LR and a closed-form expansion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}, Corollary~\ref{cor:general_L_layer_ntk}), which can be used as a starting point for finite-width corrections.

\section{Discussion}

This work leaves several natural directions open. We emphasize that our current scope is the lazy/NTK regime under explicit standard assumptions; extending beyond this setting requires additional ideas.

A recurring qualitative message, shared with depth analyses for fully trained MLPs at the edge of chaos~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc,pmlr-v163-hayou22a}, is that increasing depth tends to drive correlations toward \(1\), so layerwise kernels become nearly constant and the deep kernel approaches a rank-one structure. In RF-LR, this effect is compounded by the explicit \(1/r\) bottleneck factors in the recursion, which geometrically suppress early-layer contributions (Section~\ref{sec:depth-scaling}); consequently, after centering the relevant spectrum on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) collapses unless one rescales time/learning rate (Remark~\ref{rmk:infinite-depth-limit-appendix} in Appendix~\ref{appendix:infinite-depth-limit}).

A technical feature of our setup is that freezing the random features removes some cross-layer dependencies that appear in fully trained networks. Combined with Fisher--Kibble decoupling~\cite{fisher1915probable,hotelling1953new}, this makes certain calculations more explicit and suggests a path toward extending the analysis to joint large-\(n\) regimes using resolvent replacement and Gaussian-equivalence methods developed for shallow two-layer NTKs~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent}.


The recursion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}) suggests organizing finite-width corrections as an expansion in the bottleneck dimension \(r\). Making this program rigorous for general depth would require controlling the growth of cross-terms produced by the recursion and propagating concentration through the composed Gaussian-process layers.

On the theoretical front, several important questions remain open and constitute an immediate research agenda. Extending the exponential concentration bounds 
to general depth \( L \) layers requires controlling nested conditional means through \( L \) layers and managing a quadratic number \( O(L^2) \) of cross-terms arising
from the recursive NTK structure. The technical challenge lies in tracking how the random correlations 
\( \rho_\ell \) and norm products \( \|h^{(\ell)}(x)\|\|h^{(\ell)}(x')\| \) propagate through layers while maintaining exponential concentration rates. 
 A \textbf{non-Gaussian process propagation analysis} distinct from NNGP fits would provide complementary insights beyond the kernel regime, potentially revealing 
 how feature learning emerges from deviations from Gaussianity.
An important future direction is to establish \textbf{bulk spectral laws} for the empirical RF-LR NTK Gram matrix in a quadratic scaling regime \(n\to\infty\), adapting the Benigni--Paquette framework beyond shallow two-layer kernels to the present low-rank random-feature structure and to depth \(L\ge 2\)~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent}.

Additional theoretical extensions hold particular promise. The current analysis focuses on the three-layer case for tractability; extending to arbitrary depth \( L \) 
while maintaining explicit formulas remains an open challenge. Investigating how the edge-of-chaos (EOC) parameterization affects finite-width corrections and the \( Nr \) scaling mentioned in the architecture definition would provide concrete quantitative bounds. Understanding how the curse of dimensionality impacts normalization choices (unit norm weights vs. biases) depending on data geometry (hypercube vs. spherical) would provide principled guidance for practitioners.

The experimental validation of our theoretical predictions constitutes an equally important and concrete next step. We envision a comprehensive program that
 includes \textbf{confirming lazy-kernel predictions} and precisely mapping the boundary of lazy training in practice; conducting systematic
  \textbf{three-layer width sweeps} to test the \( O(1/n)+O(1/r) \) finite-width corrections suggested by the expansion and identify minimal \( (n,r) \)
   configurations where kernel predictions become accurate; and a detailed \textbf{comparison suite with MLPs} that probes centered conditioning and optimization time scales at matched parameter counts. These studies would verify the \textbf{exponential concentration}
    rates (Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs_rank_concentration}) and demonstrate the \textbf{practical accessibility of the kernel regime} for low-rank random features.

Finally, it would be valuable to connect the low-rank random-feature recursion studied here with broader frameworks for infinite-width limits (e.g. tensor programs~\cite{yang2020tensorprogramsiineural}) and with depth-dependent NTK spectral analyses for MLPs at the edge of chaos~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}, while maintaining explicit control of the bottleneck scaling.

Our present scope is deliberately focused on the \textbf{lazy/NTK regime}, where analytical tractability is greatest and the advantages of low-rank structure are most clearly visible. Completing finite-width formulas, spectrum characterizations, and experimental validations would strengthen the connection between the asymptotic theory and practical regimes.
