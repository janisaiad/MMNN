\section{Conclusion and discussion}

A main takeaway is that deeper networks are not necessarily unstable in the lazy regime: in the low-rank kernel setting (RF-LR), bounded \(\lambda_{\max}\) preserves step-size stability, and one can increase depth without sacrificing optimization stability while retaining the benefits of deeper approximations that one can seize. We showed that depth and rank \emph{commute} from a conditioning perspective---the explicit NTK recursion, sharp depth scaling, and condition-number bounds for the proxy kernel support this---and that low rank does not shrink the kernel function class (RKHS equivalence for three layers). The parameter budget \(O(NLr)\) thus allows trading width for depth without losing expressivity or stable step sizes, which we validated numerically for depth scaling, conditioning, and proxy--empirical concentration on equicorrelated and spherical data. In short, RF-LR offers a way to reap the benefits of depth in the NTK regime at a fraction of the parameters of full-width MLPs. We hope that this work will encourage practitioners and theorists alike to adopt low-rank structures more widely, where the parameter--depth tradeoff (with 95--99\% fewer parameters) and stability guarantees make them attractive. Proving that the same RKHS is induced for \(L\ge 4\) layers is left for future work, as are a full proxy--empirical concentration proof for arbitrary data, the connection to gradient-descent convergence and sample complexity, bulk spectral laws in the RMT scaling, and finite-width corrections.
