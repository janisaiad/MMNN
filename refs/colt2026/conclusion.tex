\section{Conclusion}

This work develops an NTK analysis for low-rank random-feature architectures (RF-LR/MMNN) under explicit assumptions. The paper contains no experiments; empirical validation is left for future work.

\textbf{Main: depth/rank scaling and condition number.} The trainable parameter budget scales as \(O(LrN)\). In the deterministic proxy mean recursion, depth drives correlations toward \(1\), the kernel magnitude saturates, and the diagonal--off-diagonal gap decays as \(\asymp 1/(rk)\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:rflr-depth-scaling-mean}). We prove a lower bound on the condition number of the \emph{proxy} Gram matrix, \(\kappa\ge\Omega(r\cdot L)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:condition-number-depth}), and one result giving exact conditioning for equicorrelated and high-dimensional random data (Corollary~\ref{cor:conditioning-equicorrelated-highdim}). Under a fixed budget, depth and rank trade off from a conditioning perspective; the choice of \(r\) and \(L\) is a matter of approximation power (e.g.\ effective depth and RKHS expressivity).

\textbf{Recursion.} We provide an explicit NTK recursion for arbitrary depth in RF-LR and a closed-form expansion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}, Corollary~\ref{cor:general_L_layer_ntk}).

\textbf{Low rank is enough for the NTK RKHS (three layers).} In the NTK regime, the three-layer RF-LR mean kernel induces the same RKHS as the shallow ReLU kernel (Corollary~\ref{thm:no_rkhs_advantage}), showing that the bottleneck does not reduce the kernel-regime function class while lowering the trainable parameter scaling from dense \(O(LN^2)\) to \(O(LrN)\).

\textbf{Concentration in the bottleneck dimension.} For the three-layer case, the empirical kernel concentrates around its deterministic limit \(K_\infty(\rho)\) with tails controlled by the bottleneck dimension \(r\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs_rank_concentration}, Corollary~\ref{cor:three_layer_ntk_concentration}). For equicorrelated data, a full proof in the appendix gives \(\|(\hat{K}-K_{\mathrm{proxy}})|_{\mathbf{1}^\perp}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(L/r+1/\sqrt{r})\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:equicorrelated-op-bound}), linking proxy and empirical conditioning on the mean-zero subspace.

\paragraph{Condition number (kernel GD).}
For squared loss in the lazy/NTK regime with mean-zero targets, stability and convergence speed depend on the condition number \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\) of the centered Gram matrix: stability requires \(\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}\), and the best fixed-step linear rate depends on \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\). Our results give a lower bound on this quantity for the proxy and, in one setting, its exact value with high probability. We do not claim to govern optimization; the link between proxy condition number and GD convergence or sample complexity is not made precise in this work.

\section{Discussion}

This work leaves several natural directions open. We emphasize that our current scope is the lazy/NTK regime under explicit standard assumptions; extending beyond this setting requires additional ideas.

A recurring qualitative message, shared with depth analyses for fully trained MLPs at the edge of chaos~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc,pmlr-v163-hayou22a}, is that increasing depth tends to drive correlations toward \(1\), so layerwise kernels become nearly constant and the deep mean kernel approaches a rank-one structure. In RF-LR, the mean-kernel infinite-depth limit collapses after centering unless one rescales time/learning rate (Remark~\ref{rmk:infinite-depth-limit-appendix} in Appendix~\ref{appendix:infinite-depth-limit}).

A technical feature of our setup is that freezing the random features removes some cross-layer dependencies that appear in fully trained networks. Combined with Fisher--Kibble decoupling~\cite{fisher1915probable,hotelling1953new}, this makes certain calculations more explicit and suggests a path toward extending the analysis to joint large-\(n\) regimes using the Benigni--Paquette framework~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent} (RMT limits in the extensive-width regime \(n\sim N^2\), layers in the RMT limit; resolvent replacement and Gaussian equivalence).


The recursion (Theorem~\ref{thm:ntk_recursion}) suggests organizing finite-width corrections as an expansion in the bottleneck dimension \(r\). Making this program rigorous for general depth would require controlling the growth of cross-terms produced by the recursion and propagating concentration through the composed Gaussian-process layers.

On the theoretical front, several important questions remain open and constitute an immediate research agenda. Extending the exponential concentration bounds 
to general depth \( L \) layers requires controlling nested conditional means through \( L \) layers and managing a quadratic number \( O(L^2) \) of cross-terms arising
from the recursive NTK structure. The technical challenge lies in tracking how the random correlations 
\( \rho_\ell \) and norm products \( \|h^{(\ell)}(x)\|\|h^{(\ell)}(x')\| \) propagate through layers while maintaining exponential concentration rates. 
 A \textbf{non-Gaussian process propagation analysis} distinct from NNGP fits would provide complementary insights beyond the kernel regime, potentially revealing 
 how feature learning emerges from deviations from Gaussianity.
An important future direction is to establish \textbf{bulk spectral laws} for the empirical RF-LR NTK Gram matrix in the RMT limit (extensive-width regime \(n\sim N^2\), \(n\to\infty\)), adapting the Benigni--Paquette framework~\cite{benigni2025eigenvaluedistributionneuraltangent} beyond shallow two-layer kernels to the present low-rank random-feature structure and to depth \(L\ge 2\).

\paragraph{Depth: what extends to general \(L\) vs.\ what remains open.}
All \textbf{conditioning} results in this paper hold for \emph{arbitrary depth} \(L\): proxy depth scaling, condition-number lower bound \(\kappa\ge\Omega(r\cdot L)\), exact conditioning for equicorrelated and high-dimensional data, and the rigorous proxy--empirical bound on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:equicorrelated-op-bound}) are proved for general \(L\). In contrast, \textbf{RKHS equivalence} (``low rank is enough'') is established only for the three-layer mean kernel; extending it to \(L\ge 4\) is open. The three-layer proof relies on a one-dimensional Fisher expectation and a \({}_2F_1\) connection formula for the Puiseux expansion near \(\rho=1\). For \(L\ge 4\), the mean kernel is an \((L-1)\)-fold expectation over a Fisher-type chain, and the conditional expectations involved differ from the unconditional mean kernel; a full proof would require controlling backward chain densities and nested hypergeometric/Laplace integrals. Possible directions include an inductive argument that the leading Puiseux exponent stays \(1/2\) at each layer or a characterization that avoids full chain marginals. See Appendix~\ref{appendix:what-extends-L} for details.

Additional theoretical extensions hold particular promise. The current analysis focuses on the three-layer case for tractability where the RKHS is concerned; extending the RKHS identification to arbitrary depth \( L \) while maintaining explicit formulas remains an open challenge. Investigating how the edge-of-chaos (EOC) parameterization affects finite-width corrections and the \( Nr \) scaling mentioned in the architecture definition would provide concrete quantitative bounds. Understanding how the curse of dimensionality impacts normalization choices (unit norm weights vs. biases) depending on data geometry (hypercube vs. spherical) would provide principled guidance for practitioners.

The experimental validation of our theoretical predictions constitutes an equally important and concrete next step. We envision a comprehensive program that
 includes \textbf{confirming lazy-kernel predictions} and precisely mapping the boundary of lazy training in practice; conducting systematic
  \textbf{three-layer width sweeps} to test the \( O(1/n)+O(1/r) \) finite-width corrections suggested by the expansion and identify minimal \( (n,r) \)
   configurations where kernel predictions become accurate; and a detailed \textbf{comparison suite with MLPs} that probes centered conditioning and optimization time scales at matched parameter counts. These studies would verify the \textbf{exponential concentration}
    rates (Theorem~\ref{thm:rkhs_rank_concentration}) and demonstrate the \textbf{practical accessibility of the kernel regime} for low-rank random features.

Finally, it would be valuable to connect the low-rank random-feature recursion studied here with broader frameworks for infinite-width limits (e.g. tensor programs~\cite{yang2020tensorprogramsiineural}) and with depth-dependent NTK spectral analyses for MLPs at the edge of chaos~\cite{terjek2025mlpsateoc}, while maintaining explicit control of the bottleneck scaling.

Our present scope is deliberately focused on the \textbf{lazy/NTK regime}, where analytical tractability is greatest and the advantages of low-rank structure are most clearly visible. Completing finite-width formulas, spectrum characterizations, and experimental validations would strengthen the connection between the asymptotic theory and practical regimes.
