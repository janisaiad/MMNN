\begin{abstract}
We study \textbf{feature learning} and its \textbf{relation to the loss landscape} for low-rank random feature (RF-LR) networks in frequency-dependent, 1D regression. We combine: (1) a tractable mean-field picture and a rigorous \emph{log-ratio criterion} for channel dominance, with experimental validation and diagnostics (log-ratio trajectories, specialization vs.\ collapse by LR/batch); (2) an \emph{empirical study of oscillatory complexity} across layers via the mean number of strict local minima per channel (layer-wise partial functions), showing that deeper layers learn more oscillatory representations; (3) the \emph{landscape--feature link} and \emph{valley--frequency correspondence}: plateau--sharp--plateau loss structure where each plateau corresponds to a frequency to be learned (saddle-to-saddle dynamics), sharp deep basins (valleys) that are hard to enter unless the learning rate is small (loss and partials before/after LR divides), scaling of plateau-escape time with LR and batch size, and symmetry preservation (low-rank preserves target symmetry, full-rank can break it). We provide a single, reproducible methodology and config index. Scope: 1D regression and the cos functions/RF-LR setting. This submission fits the workshop focus on empirical studies of learned representations and loss landscapes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

We study how \emph{low-rank random feature} (RF-LR) networks learn representations and how this ties to the loss landscape \cite{chizat2018globalconvergencegradientdescent,abbe2023sgdlearningneuralnetworks} in 1D regression on frequency-dependent (cos) targets.

Our contribution is a \textbf{unified picture} in three strands. \textbf{(i) Feature learning and the log-ratio:} channels specialize to distinct spatial locations and progressively capture higher frequencies; a mean-field toy model \cite{nguyen2023rigorousframeworkmeanfield,pham2021globalconvergencethreelayerneural} and a conditional \emph{log-ratio criterion} characterize when one channel dominates; we validate with log-ratio trajectories and diagnostics (collapse vs.\ convergence with LR/batch). \textbf{(ii) Oscillatory complexity:} the mean number of strict local minima per channel (layer-wise partials) increases with layer index, consistent with deeper layers learning more oscillatory representations. \textbf{(iii) Valley--frequency correspondence:} the loss has plateau--sharp--plateau structure (saddle-to-saddle); each plateau is a frequency not yet learned; the landscape is a plateau with sharp, deep valleys reachable only when LR is small---at each LR divide the loss drops and partials refine; we report plateau-escape scaling with LR and batch and show that low-rank preserves target symmetry while full-rank can learn asymmetric features. Observables (mean minima per channel, $2L$-spike pattern per layer $L$) bridge landscape and feature learning. Scope: 1D cos functions/RF-LR; we do not claim generalization beyond this setting.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

We work with \textbf{low-rank random feature (RF-LR)} networks \cite{zhang2025structuredbalancedmulticomponentmultilayer} on 1D regression with cosine targets. The architecture uses a tall-skinny bottleneck: the first-layer features $L^0$ and the mixing matrices $L^{(\ell)}$ are frozen, and only the channel weights $w_\ell$ are trained. Target, data, and the full training protocol are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:setup}.

\subsection{Theory: when does a channel dominate?}
\label{sec:theory}

When a single channel dominates at some input, the backward pass tends to reinforce that dominance (sign-coherence and a self-reinforcing loop). We formalize this with a conditional \textbf{log-ratio criterion}: under certain conditions on an interval $I$, once a channel dominates there it cannot lose that role on $I$. The two-sided step toy model and the precise statement appear in Appendix~\ref{app:toy}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

When trained with SGD, the loss curve spends long stretches on flat plateaus before dropping sharply; each plateau corresponds to a frequency that the network has not yet captured, and the dynamics move from saddle to saddle \cite{abbe2023sgdlearningneuralnetworks}. The loss landscape effectively presents a plateau riddled with narrow valleys: the optimizer wanders on the plateau until a learning-rate reduction allows it to slip into one of these valleys. When that happens, the loss drops and the fit improves, with high-frequency structure emerging in the middle of the input domain and in the channel partials (Figures~\ref{fig:loss_fit_L2}, \ref{fig:partial_L3}). As each new frequency is acquired, the partial functions become more oscillatory, and the mean number of strict local minima per channel goes up.

This pattern is consistent with a landscape that has many deep, narrow valleys: the optimizer can reach near machine-precision loss in one of them and then move out again.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our results are limited to 1D regression with cosine targets and the RF-LR architecture; we do not claim that the same feature--landscape relations hold in higher dimensions or for other architectures. The remarks on Adam and on scaling are qualitative, and the oscillatory-complexity numbers come from single runs per configuration. Very deep networks can become unstable. Full configurations and methodology are given in the Appendix.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented a unified picture of feature learning and the loss landscape for RF-LR networks in 1D. The log-ratio criterion and toy model clarify when a channel dominates, and the diagnostics (trajectories, mean minima per channel) support this picture empirically. The loss evolves in a plateau--sharp--plateau fashion, with sharp basins that become accessible when the learning rate is reduced, and observables such as mean minima per channel and the $2L$-spike pattern link the geometry of the landscape to the way features are learned. Low-rank structure also preserves the symmetry of the target. Within this setting, understanding the loss landscape amounts to understanding when and how the network acquires each frequency.

\section{Setup: architecture, target, data, and training}
\label{app:setup}

\subsection{Architecture and low-rank structure}
The \textbf{low-rank random feature (RF-LR)} network has input/output dimension $1$ and $L$ hidden blocks. Each block is: (i) linear rank$\to$width, (ii) ReLU, (iii) linear width$\to$rank, with \emph{rank} $r \ll$ \emph{width} $n$ (tall-skinny bottleneck). Factorized view: weight matrix $W = L\,R^\top$, $r \ll n$; first-layer features $L^0$ and mixing matrices $L^{(\ell)}$ are \textbf{frozen}; only channel weights $w_\ell$ (width$\to$rank linears) are trained. With \textbf{fixWb = True}: first linear per block frozen. Main configs: $W=1024$, $R=10$, $L \in \{2,3\}$; $\mu$-parameterization; SGD, momentum $0$, gradient clipping 1.0. Channel partial functions $f_k$: scalar outputs per channel; evolution in Appendix~\ref{app:toy}.

\subsection{Target, data, and training}
\textbf{Target:} $y(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{factor}} \cos(2\pi k x)$ on $x \in [-1,1]$. \textbf{Data:} uniform grid of $N_{\mathrm{train}}$ points; main figures: $N_{\mathrm{train}}=768$, batch size $8$. Loss: MSE. \textbf{Training:} initial LR $10^{-2}$; adaptive stagnation (LR halved when loss does not improve over 10 epochs, min 20 between reductions; stop if LR $< 10^{-6}$). Checkpoints: before and 10 epochs after each LR divide. Max 10,000 epochs. Full config naming and sweeps: Appendix~\ref{app:methodology}.

\subsection{Symmetry and log-ratio figure setups}
Symmetry (Figure~\ref{fig:mlp-asymmetry}): 8 layers, $n=1024$, $r=20$, target $(f_1,f_2)=(144,48)$, $N=4k$, Adam, batch 100. Log-ratio diagnostics: 3-layer, $n=1024$, $r=15$, target $\cos(8\pi x)$, $N=5000$; divergence/convergence: lr $2{\times}10^{-3}$ bs 4 vs.\ lr $2{\times}10^{-4}$ bs 4. Oscillatory complexity: $L=6$, $W=128$, $R=5$; mean minima on layer-$\ell$ partials above $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$.

\section{Two-sided step toy model and log-ratio criterion}
\label{app:toy}

\subsection{Setup and closed-form dynamics}

Consider the \emph{two-sided step}: two support points $x_0=-\delta$, $x_1=+\delta$ ($\delta>0$) with $y(x_0)=+A$, $y(x_1)=-A$. For this finitely supported data, define $d_0(t)=d_L((x_0,y(x_0));W(t))$, $d_1(t)=d_L((x_1,y(x_1));W(t))$, $B_{k,1}(t)=B_k(t;x_0)$, $B_{k,2}(t)=B_k(t;x_1)$, and
\[
\Gamma_{k,1}(t) = \int_0^t \xi_1(s)\,d_0(s)\,B_{k,1}(s)\,ds,\qquad \Gamma_{k,2}(t) = \int_0^t \xi_1(s)\,d_1(s)\,B_{k,2}(s)\,ds.
\]
Then $f_k$ takes the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fk-twostep}
f_k(t,x) = f_k(0,x) - \Gamma_{k,1}(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x,x_0) - \Gamma_{k,2}(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x,x_1).
\end{equation}
The spike shape is determined by $K_{\mu_0}$ (NNGP, \cite{jacot2018neural}); all learning dynamics reduce to the scalar coefficients $\Gamma_{k,1}(t)$, $\Gamma_{k,2}(t)$. The kernel satisfies $K_{\mu_0}(x_p,x_p)=K_0>0$ for $p\in\{0,1\}$ (same by symmetry). The off-diagonal $K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_1)=K_{\mu_0}(-\delta,+\delta)$ is positive and fastly decaying in $\delta$: $0<K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_1)\le \psi(\delta)$ with $\psi(\delta)\to 0$ rapidly as $\delta\to\infty$. Thus the cross-term is small for separated points and, being positive, reinforces the leading local term in the log-ratio dynamics.

The full evolution has two terms (local plus non-local). At $x_0$ and $x_1$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dtfk-full-delta}
\partial_t f_k(t,x_0) = -\xi_1(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_0)\,d_0(t)\,B_{k,1}(t) + E_0(t),\quad \partial_t f_k(t,x_1) = -\xi_1(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x_1,x_1)\,d_1(t)\,B_{k,2}(t) + E_1(t),
\end{equation}
where the non-local remainders satisfy $|E_p(t)|\le C'\,\psi(\delta)$ with $\psi(\delta)$ fastly decaying in $\delta$.

\subsection{Statement: log-ratio criterion and theorem}

\begin{definition}[Log-ratio criterion]
At $x_0$, define $R_{12}(t,x_0) = \log\frac{|f_1(t,x_0)|}{|f_2(t,x_0)|}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided step: log-ratio growth at $x_0$ (conditional)]
\label{thm:logratio}
\textbf{Setting:} The two-sided step with $r=2$ channels and a 3-layer RF-LR; the dynamics are \eqref{eq:fk-twostep} and the full evolution \eqref{eq:dtfk-full-delta}. The result is \textbf{conditional}: \emph{if} the three conditions in (Hypothesis) hold on an interval $I \subseteq [0,\infty)$, \emph{then} the stated conclusion holds on $I$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis (at $x_0$, for all $t\in I$):} (i)~$-d_0(t)\ge 0$; (ii)~$B_{1,1}(t)$ has the same sign as $f_1(t,x_0)$; (iii)~there exists $\rho_0\in[0,1)$ such that $|B_{2,1}(t)|\le \rho_0\,\frac{|f_2(t,x_0)|}{|f_1(t,x_0)|}\,|B_{1,1}(t)|$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion:} For $t\in I$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0) &= (1-\rho_0)\,\xi_1(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_0)\,(-d_0(t))\,\frac{|B_{1,1}(t)|}{|f_1(t,x_0)|} + \varepsilon_0(t),
\end{aligned}
\]
with $|\varepsilon_0(t)|\le C''\,\psi(\delta)$ for $\psi(\delta)$ fastly decaying in $\delta$ (from $E_p$). Hence $\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0)\ge 0$ whenever the leading term dominates $|\varepsilon_0|$; since $K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_1)>0$, the off-diagonal coupling contributes with a favorable sign and reinforces the leading term. Strict dominance of channel~1 at $x_0$ cannot be lost on $I$ and is amplified whenever $|B_{1,1}|$ is not too small. The result is symmetrical at $x_1$ (channel~2 dominates there).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[On the hypothesis]
The theorem does \emph{not} assert that (i)--(iii) hold for the canonical two-sided step ($y=\pm A$ at $x=\pm\delta$) from generic initial conditions; it only establishes that \emph{when} they hold on $I$, the conclusion follows. Verifying (i)--(iii) from the ODEs for specific initial conditions and $A,\delta$ is outside the scope of this result. When (i)--(iii) hold in practice is discussed below.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:logratio}}

We prove the theorem at $x_0$; the argument at $x_1$ is symmetrical.

\paragraph{At $x_0$.}
By the log-ratio derivative identity at $x_0$,
\[
\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0)=\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_1)\,\partial_t f_1}{|f_1|}-\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_2)\,\partial_t f_2}{|f_2|}.
\]
Insert the full evolution \eqref{eq:dtfk-full-delta}: $\partial_t f_k(t,x_0)=-\xi_1\,K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_0)\,d_0\,B_{k,1}+E_0(t)$ with $|E_0(t)|\le C'\,\psi(\delta)$ for $\psi(\delta)$ fastly decaying in $\delta$. Then
\[
\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0)=\xi_1(t)\,K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_0)\,(-d_0(t))\Big(
\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_1)B_{1,1}}{|f_1|}-\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_2)B_{2,1}}{|f_2|}\Big)
+ \varepsilon_0(t),\qquad |\varepsilon_0(t)|\le C''\,\psi(\delta),
\]
where $\varepsilon_0(t)=E_0(t)\,\big(\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_1)}{|f_1|}-\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_2)}{|f_2|}\big)$ inherits the bound from $E_0$. Use $-d_0\ge 0$. The sign condition gives $\mathrm{sign}(f_1)B_{1,1}=|B_{1,1}|$; $-\mathrm{sign}(f_2)B_{2,1}\ge -|B_{2,1}|$. The dominance inequality at $x_0$ is $|B_{2,1}|\le \rho_0\,\frac{|f_2|}{|f_1|}\,|B_{1,1}|$, hence
\[
\frac{|B_{1,1}|}{|f_1|}-\frac{\mathrm{sign}(f_2)B_{2,1}}{|f_2|}
\ge \frac{|B_{1,1}|}{|f_1|}-\frac{|B_{2,1}|}{|f_2|}
\ge (1-\rho_0)\,\frac{|B_{1,1}|}{|f_1|},
\]
so $\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0)= (1-\rho_0)\,\xi_1\,K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_0)\,(-d_0)\,\frac{|B_{1,1}|}{|f_1|}+\varepsilon_0(t)$. The leading term is $\ge 0$; since $K_{\mu_0}(x_0,x_1)>0$ and fastly decaying, the off-diagonal coupling reinforces the leading term while $|\varepsilon_0|\le C''\,\psi(\delta)$ is negligible for $\delta$ large. Thus $\partial_t R_{12}(t,x_0)\ge 0$ whenever the leading term dominates $|\varepsilon_0|$.

\paragraph{At $x_1$.}
The same argument applies by symmetry (indices $1\leftrightarrow 2$, $x_0\leftrightarrow x_1$): channel~2 dominates at $x_1$ and $\partial_t R_{21}(t,x_1)\ge 0$.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The log-ratio $R_{12}$ at $x_0$ is non-decreasing on $I$; strict dominance of channel~1 at $x_0$ cannot be lost and is amplified whenever $|B_{1,1}|$ is not too small. By symmetry, channel~2 dominates at $x_1$.

\subsection{On the hypothesis: when (i)--(iii) hold in practice}

The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:logratio} is conditional: (i)~$-d_0(t)\ge 0$, (ii)~$B_{1,1}(t)$ has the same sign as $f_1(t,x_0)$, and (iii)~$|B_{2,1}(t)|\le \rho_0\,\frac{|f_2(t,x_0)|}{|f_1(t,x_0)|}\,|B_{1,1}(t)|$ for some $\rho_0\in[0,1)$. In practice, these conditions are observed to hold in standard training setups. Under \emph{Xavier initialization} (or similar scale-corrected schemes) for the frozen feature and mixing matrices, and \emph{sub-Gaussian initialization} for the trainable weights $w_1$, $w_2$, the initial $f_k$ and backprop signals $B_{k,p}$ are well-balanced across channels. The gradient-flow dynamics then tend to amplify small asymmetries: once one channel leads at $x_0$, (ii)~and (iii)~are maintained because the dominant channel receives a larger $B_k$ and thus a larger $\partial_t f_k$, while the weaker channel's backprop stays proportionally smaller. Condition~(i) holds when the network under-predicts at $x_0$; for squared loss $d_L \propto \hat{y}-y$, $-d_0\ge 0$ corresponds to $\hat{y}(x_0)\le y(x_0)=+A$, which is typical before convergence. Thus, although the theorem does not prove (i)--(iii) from first principles, they are consistent with and typically observed under standard initialization.

\subsection{Plateau-escape and log-ratio runs}
\label{app:plateau-logratio}

Runs with fixed lr and batch size: train until loss $<$ threshold; record \textbf{epochs to escape} and \textbf{log-ratio} $R_{i,j}=\log|f_i|-\log|f_j|$ at $x=0$ over time. Main-figure setting: factor 3, $N=768$, $L=3$, rank 10, width 1024; escape threshold 0.1; max 400--2000 epochs. Log-ratio trajectory figures: lr $2{\times}10^{-3}$ bs 4 (divergence) and lr $2{\times}10^{-4}$ bs 4 (convergence). Epochs-to-escape scaling (Figures in main text): sweep over lr and batch size; scaling consistent with $\propto 1/\mathrm{lr}$ and small batch (1--4) escaping effectively.

\subsection{Frequency/layer-scaling}
Multi-frequency cosine target; freq multipliers 0.3, 0.6, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10; ranks 10, 15, 25; layer counts 3, 5, 8, 12, 16, 24, 40, 56, 80. 58 completed configs. Outcomes: low freq (0.3, 0.6) test error $\sim 10^{-6}$--$10^{-5}$; high freq (1.5+) no convergence; very deep ($L=56$, 80) blow-up or NaN at higher freq (e.g.\ freq$\times$5, 7).

\subsection{Symmetry comparison}
Full-rank MLP (fixWb = False): 8 layers, width 1024, $r=20$, target $f(x)=\cos(f_1\pi x^2)-0.8\cos(f_2\pi x^2)$ on $[-1,1]$ with $(f_1,f_2)=(144,48)$; $N=4k$, 10k epochs, Adam lr 0.001, batch 100, StepLR $\gamma=0.9$ every 100. Channel partials at layers 7 and 16 show asymmetry about $x=0$. Low-rank (RF-LR) with same hyperparameters preserves symmetry.

\subsection{Oscillatory-complexity (partial-function minima)}
Per-layer partial functions on a uniform grid; count of strict local minima above a positivity threshold per channel; layer statistic = mean minima per channel. Outputs: per-layer and per-group stats; figure mean minima per channel vs.\ layer (e.g.\ $L=6$, $W=128$, $R=5$).
