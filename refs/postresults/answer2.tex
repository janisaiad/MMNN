Rebuttal (Reviewer 2)

We thank the reviewer for the careful and substantive feedback. Below we provide consolidated responses to each point.

Q1 (Novelty, freezing limitation, and scope of Theorem 4.1):  
We agree with the core concern that our proof template is related to Nguyen & Pham (2023), and that the two-point theorem is a minimal rigorous instance rather than a full high-dimensional representation-learning theory. Our intended contribution is the architecture-level shift and its optimization consequences: frozen low-rank mixing yields a closed deep mean-field description with channel-resolved dynamics and avoids the fully symmetric “same update for all neurons” failure mode highlighted in full-rank i.i.d. settings.  
Technically, the key non-verbatim step is the channelized forward/backward structure:
H_ell = sum_{k=1}^r W^{(ell)}_{.,k} f_k^{(ell)},
with stability/coupling controlled by ||W^{(ell)}||_{infty,1} and channel maxima.  
We will also make scope explicit in the camera-ready: this is not meant to cover all end-to-end modern deep learning pipelines; it targets a structured low-rank regime relevant to scientific ML/PDE settings.  
For Theorem 4.1, we will keep the two-point theorem as the formal anchor and add a clear mechanism remark (positive channel feedback through ReLU gating), while labeling high-dimensional extension claims as empirical/ongoing rather than proved.

Q2 (Symmetry sensitivity and exponential factor in finite-width bound):  
We agree this is important. The exponential prefactor in the finite-width bound (schematically exp(K_T(1+rK))) is a conservative Gronwall artifact and should not be interpreted as a direct causal predictor of observed symmetry outcomes.  
Empirically, symmetry behavior is setup-dependent (target, batch size, initialization, seed). We therefore added variance-aware reporting and avoid universal claims such as “rank 10 works, rank 20 fails.” In our post-review sweep (fixed width 1024, five seeds, synthetic even target), rank 20 showed lower mean symmetry defect than rank 10 while test MSE remained comparable within noise.  
We will present symmetry as a protocol-dependent observation/hypothesis, not as a theorem-level guarantee.

Q3 (Scaling with intrinsic dimension and phase-transition question):  
We agree this is a key open direction. Our current mean-field guarantees are for fixed rank r in the infinite-width limit; they do not by themselves provide a full phase diagram in (d, r, N).  
We will state this limitation explicitly.  
On ongoing directions:  
- kernel/NTK side: structured low-rank kernel concentration in high dimension,  
- mean-field scaling side: rank-vs-width regimes where ODE descriptions remain faithful,  
- empirical side: controlled synthetic sweeps over (d, r, N) to test transition behavior.  
We also clarify the “exponential suppression” intuition: the ReLU failure mode corresponds to joint dead-gate sign alignment; heuristically, the input-region measure where all channels are dead can shrink exponentially in r (under symmetric mixing assumptions). Whether this remains effective as d grows depends on how representation requirements (r) and gating/concentration geometry co-scale.

Q4 (Mechanism beyond the two-point toy theorem):  
We will include an explicit mechanism paragraph in the camera-ready:  
- low-rank pre-activation H2(c2; x, W) = sum_{j=1}^r W_{c2,j} f_j(x; W),  
- ReLU gate indicator 1{H2>0} couples channel strength and activation,  
- dominant channel alignment increases drift on that channel, creating a reinforcing loop.  
This is the conceptual mechanism we intend to communicate; the two-point theorem remains the minimal formal proof instance.

Q5 (Limitation wording around frozen mixing):  
We agree the limitation must be framed carefully. We will revise the text to a precise scope statement: frozen mixing is an architectural assumption of the analyzed class, not a claim that all practical deep networks should be modeled this way.  
At the same time, we will keep the positive message explicit: low-rank/factorized models can retain strong optimization guarantees in the mean-field regime; this is a meaningful design point rather than merely a restrictive toy case.
