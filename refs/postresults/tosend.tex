We thank the reviewer for the detailed and constructive comments. Below we answer the main clarification requests by stating the intended meaning and the concrete definitions.

**Sec. 2 (citation style).** Agreed: the current draft mixes author-name and numeric citations. We will make the style consistent throughout.

**Sec. 3.1 (frozen RF; $\mathrm{supp}(W^0(C_1))$).** The phrase "$\mathrm{supp}(W^0(C_1))$" was imprecise. What is used is a standard RF richness assumption: the linear span of $\{\phi_1(\langle W^0(c_1),\cdot\rangle)\}$ is dense in a target class (e.g. $L^2(\mu_X)$). This richness is only assumed at the frozen layer $\ell=0$; for trained layers $\ell>1$ we do not assume "full support in $\mathbb{R}^d$".

**Mean-field width limit.** Yes: this is the infinite-width limit under mean-field scaling (neuronal embedding), i.e. $n_1,\dots,n_L\to\infty$ so empirical neuron averages converge to expectations under limiting neuron measures (not NTK/lazy scaling; not maximal update parameterization).

**Sec. 3.1 (matrix form).** Yes: it is clearer to also write $x'=\varphi(W^\top A x + b)$ (equivalently $R=W^\top A$, $d=W^\top c+b$), alongside the channel form $H_2(c_2;x)=\sum_{k=1}^r W_{c_2,k} f_k(x)$.

**Sec. 3.2 (notation $c_1/C_1$, expectations).** $C_i$ is a random neuron label with law $\rho_i$; $\mathbb{E}_{C_i}[\cdot]$ is integration under $\rho_i$. Finite width corresponds to i.i.d. samples $C_i(j)\sim\rho_i$, so empirical averages converge to $\mathbb{E}_{C_i}[\cdot]$. This is equivalent to the usual pushforward-measure formulation on Euclidean weights; we will remove the misleading phrase "indices become continuous" and harmonize $c_i/C_i$ notation.

**Sec. 3.3 (loss; gradient flow; schedules).** The population loss is $\mathcal{L}(W)=\mathbb{E}_Z[\ell(Y,\hat y(X;W))]$, and the mean-field ODE is its gradient flow:
$\partial_t W^{(\ell)}(t)=-\xi_\ell(t)\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}}\mathcal{L}(W(t))$,
with layer-wise learning-rate schedules $\xi_\ell(t)\ge0$. Euler/SGD discretization reads $W_{n+1}=W_n-\varepsilon\,\xi_\ell(\varepsilon n)\nabla\mathcal{L}(W_n)$.

**Sec. 3.4 ("better than null"; ReLU; meaning of $r$; mixing).** "Better than null" means $\mathcal{L}(W(0))<\mathbb{E}[\ell(Y,\phi_L(0))]$ (beats the trivial constant predictor). Here $r$ is the channel rank (number of channels). The global-optimality implication uses $\inf_u|\phi_2'(u)|>0$ (so plain ReLU is excluded); for ReLU, the obstruction is the dead-gate case ($\phi_2'(H_2)=0$ while the residual is nonzero). "Mixing" refers to the (frozen) channel recombination matrices $W^{(\ell)}$. A sufficient bounded-mixing condition is: $|W^{(\ell)}_{c,k}|\le K$ and $||W^{(\ell)}||_{\infty,1}:=\sup_c\sum_{k=1}^r |W^{(\ell)}_{c,k}|\le rK$.

**Sec. 3.5-3.8 (fixing the notations, including $D$).** The Lipschitz display around $|H_\ell(W')-H_\ell(W'')|$ was confusing; in our setting $W^{(\ell)}$ is the frozen mixing at layer $\ell$, and the difference between $W'$ and $W''$ enters through the channel features. A typical corrected bound is
$|H_\ell(W')-H_\ell(W'')|\le ||W^{(\ell)}||_{\infty,1}\max_{k\le r}|f_k(W')-f_k(W'')|$.
We define the solution operator $F$ as the Picard map $(Fw)(t)=w(0)+\int_0^t\Psi(w(s))\,ds$. We replace "upstream x local" by "backpropagated signal x local derivative", and $\partial_2\mathcal{L}$ means the partial derivative w.r.t. the prediction argument.
Regarding "shifts": this refers to the neuron-independent translation collapse mode in deep i.i.d. limits; RF-LR yields heterogeneous updates because frozen mixing breaks that symmetry.

**Theorem 3.2 vs 3.3; $(n_1,n_2)$; coupling; what is $D$.** In Theorem 3.3, $(n_1,n_2)$ are exactly the numbers of neurons in the first and second hidden layers (depth-2 quantitative theorem). This is why only a 2-tuple appears; for depth $L$ one would index by $(n_1,\dots,n_L)$.
"Coupling procedure" does not refer to an extra algorithm: it is the standard neuronal-embedding coupling viewpoint where neurons are labeled by i.i.d. draws $C_i(j)\sim\rho_i$ and one compares empirical neuron averages to the mean-field expectations.
The symbol $D$ in the informal statement is shorthand for the concrete discrepancy used in the bound, namely a sup-norm trajectory discrepancy $\mathscr{D}_T$ on $[0,T]$. Concretely (depth 2), one may take
$\mathscr{D}_T(W,\mathbf{W}) := \sup_{t\le T}\max\{\max_{j_1\le n_1,\,k\le r}|w_1(t,C_1(j_1),k)-\mathbf{w}_1(\lfloor t/\varepsilon\rfloor,j_1,k)|,\ \max_{j_2\le n_2}|w_2(t,C_2(j_2))-\mathbf{w}_2(\lfloor t/\varepsilon\rfloor,j_2)|\}$.
The quantitative bound scales as $O(1/\sqrt{n_{\min}}+\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ (up to the usual Gronwall-type prefactor).

Q1 (Convergence guarantees). Our main guarantee is conditional on convergence of the mean-field dynamics: given convergence, we can conclude global optimality within the RF-LR class under the stated assumptions. Q2 (High-dimensional spike mechanism). The ODE-level feedback behind channel specialization is dimension-agnostic at the algebraic level: it is driven by the same identity $H_2(c_2;x,W)=\sum_{j=1}^r W_{c_2,j} f_j(x;W)$ together with gating/backprop terms.
To make the mechanism more transparent, we will add a short explanation in the main text. For ReLU-type gates, the derivative term is an indicator $\mathbf 1\{H_2>0\}$, so the channel-$k$ drift/backprop signal is built from expectations over $C_2$ of terms that couple (i) the mixing weight $W_{C_2,k}$, (ii) the gate $\mathbf 1\{H_2(C_2;x,W)>0\}$, and (iii) upstream factors (output error and later-layer weights). When one channel becomes slightly dominant on part of the input space, it increases $H_2$ there, turns on more gates for neurons aligned with that channel, and thereby increases the channel-$k$ drift. This in turn pushes $f_k$ further in the same direction: a reinforcing feedback loop. The two-point theorem is a minimal tractable instance where this loop can be proved cleanly.
What we currently prove is a minimal two-point anchor; for general $x\in\mathbb R^d$, overlapping channel contributions complicate the rigorous isolation of “well-separated spikes”. We will state this distinction clearly, keep the high-$d$ discussion honest as primarily empirical, and add higher-$d$ experiments/diagnostics as space permits. We will also note that $d=1$ is used mainly for visualization clarity; the algebraic structure above does not rely on $d=1$.
For ReLU specifically, we will also clarify the main degenerate failure mode: convergence to a stationary point where gates are off on a large set (so $\phi_2'(H_2)=0$ despite nonzero residual). In RF-LR, $H_2(c_2;x,W)=\sum_{k=1}^r W_{c_2,k} f_k(x;W)$ is a sum over $r$ channels, so the event that all channels are simultaneously “dead” is a joint sign-alignment event across channels. Under symmetric random mixing and non-degenerate channel features, one can heuristically view this as exponentially unlikely in $r$ (e.g. on the order of $2^{-r}$ pointwise), which motivates the “high probability in $r$” intuition. We will keep this strictly as intuition (not as a proof step) and will be careful about how it is stated.

Q3 (Sensitivity to the frozen draw). The analytical conditions are not tied to one sampling recipe: once mixing matrices are frozen, the arguments use bounded mixing and a richness/diversity assumption for the frozen first-layer features, not Gaussianity. Concretely, a convenient sufficient form is: for all $c,k$, $|W^{(\ell)}_{c,k}|\le K$, hence $||W^{(\ell)}||_{\infty,1}:=\sup_c\sum_{k=1}^r |W^{(\ell)}_{c,k}|\le rK$ almost surely. This can be satisfied by any bounded-support mixing distribution (and is also compatible with nonnegative/NMF-style mixing); there is no Gaussian-specific requirement in the theory.
At finite width, performance can vary with the frozen draw. In our current experiments we often use Gaussian/Xavier initializations for convenience, even though those are unbounded; in the camera-ready we will clearly separate the theorem assumptions from this engineering choice, and where feasible we will include multi-draw variability (multiple frozen draws, multiple seeds) in addition to the usual seed variability.
