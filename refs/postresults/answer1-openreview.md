**Sec. 2 (citation style).** Agreed, we will make the style consistent throughout.

**Sec. 3.1 (frozen RF; richness / support phrasing).** The informal “$\mathrm{supp}(W^0(C\_1))$” wording was imprecise. What we use is a standard random-feature **richness** assumption: the linear span of $\bigl\lbrace \phi\_1\bigl(\langle W^0(c\_1),\cdot\rangle\bigr) \bigr\rbrace$ is dense in a suitable target class (e.g. $L^2(\mu\_X)$). This richness is only required at the **frozen** first layer ($\ell=0$). For trained layers $\ell>1$ we do **not** require a “full support in $\mathbb{R}^d$” statement for the trained objects.

**Mean-field width limit.** Yes: this means the infinite-width limit under **mean-field scaling** (neuronal embedding), i.e. widths $n\_1,\dots,n\_L\to\infty$ so empirical neuron averages converge to expectations under limiting neuron measures. This is **not** the NTK/lazy scaling and **not** maximal update parameterization (Hayou & Yang et al).

**Sec. 3.1 (matrix form).** We can rewrite $x'=\varphi(W^\top A x+b)$ with $R:=W^\top A$ and $d:=W^\top c+b$, alongside the channel form $H\_2(c\_2;x)=\sum\_{k=1}^r W\_{c\_2,k}\, f\_k(x)$. But this lacks low rank channel interpretration.

**Sec. 3.2 (notation and expectations).** $C\_i$ is a random neuron label with law $\rho\_i$; $\mathbb{E}\_{C\_i}[\cdot]$ is integration under $\rho\_i$. At finite width, labels are i.i.d. samples $C\_i(j)\sim\rho\_i$, so empirical averages converge to $\mathbb{E}\_{C\_i}[\cdot]$ under mean-field scaling. This matches the usual pushforward-measure picture on Euclidean weights. We will remove the misleading phrase “indices become continuous” and harmonize $c\_i/C\_i$ notation.

**Sec. 3.3 (loss; gradient flow; schedules).** The population loss is $\mathcal{L}(W)=\mathbb{E}\_Z\bigl[\ell\bigl(Y,\hat{y}(X;W)\bigr)\bigr]$. The mean-field ODE is $\partial\_t W^{(\ell)}(t)=-\xi\_{\ell}(t)\,\nabla\_{W^{(\ell)}}\mathcal{L}\bigl(W(t)\bigr)$ with layer-wise schedules $\xi\_{\ell}(t)\ge 0$, and Euler / SGD reads $W\_{n+1}=W\_n-\varepsilon\,\xi\_{\ell}(\varepsilon n)\,\nabla\mathcal{L}(W\_n)$.

**Sec. 3.4 (“better than null”; ReLU; mixing bounds).** “Better than null” means $\mathcal{L}(W(0))<\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ell\bigl(Y,\phi\_L(0)\bigr)\bigr]$ (strict improvement over the trivial constant predictor). Here $r$ is the **channel rank** (number of channels). The formal global-optimality step uses $\inf\_u\lvert\phi\_2'(u)\rvert>0$, so **plain ReLU is excluded** in that theorem; for ReLU the obstruction is the dead-gate regime ($\phi\_2'(H\_2)=0$ with nonzero residual). “Mixing” means the (frozen) channel recombination matrices $W^{(\ell)}$. A convenient sufficient bound is $\lvert W^{(\ell)}\_{c,k}\rvert\le K$ and $\lVert W^{(\ell)}\rVert\_{\infty,1}:=\sup\_{c}\sum\_{k=1}^r \lvert W^{(\ell)}\_{c,k}\rvert\le rK$.

**Sec. 3.5–3.8 (Lipschitz display; Picard map; wording).** The Lipschitz display around $\lvert H\_{\ell}(W')-H\_{\ell}(W'')\rvert$ was confusing: $W^{(\ell)}$ denotes **frozen** mixing, and differences in trajectories enter through **channel features**. A typical corrected form is $\lvert H\_{\ell}(W')-H\_{\ell}(W'')\rvert\le \lVert W^{(\ell)}\rVert\_{\infty,1}\,\max\_{k\le r}\bigl\lvert f\_k(W')-f\_k(W'')\bigr\rvert$. We define the solution operator $F$ as the Picard map $(Fw)(t)=w(0)+\int\_0^t \Psi(w(s))\,\mathrm{d}s$. We replace “upstream $\times$ local” by “backpropagated signal $\times$ local derivative,” and $\partial\_2\mathcal{L}$ means the partial derivative w.r.t. the **prediction** argument. “Shifts” refer to the neuron-independent translation-collapse mode in deep i.i.d. full-rank limits (see Nguyen et al section 5.1.2); RF-LR breaks that symmetry via frozen mixing and yields heterogeneous updates across neurons.

**Theorem 3.2 vs 3.3 (hidden widths; coupling; symbol D).** In Theorem 3.3, $(n\_1,n\_2)$ are exactly the hidden widths in the **depth-2** quantitative bound—hence a pair only; for depth $L$ one would use $(n\_1,\dots,n\_L)$.

“Coupling procedure” is **not** an extra algorithm: it is the neuronal-embedding viewpoint (i.i.d. labels $C\_i(j)\sim\rho\_i$, compare empirical neuron averages to mean-field expectations).

The informal symbol $D$ denotes a concrete **sup-norm trajectory discrepancy** $\mathscr{D}\_T$ on $[0,T]$. For depth 2, writing $d\_1(t):=\max\_{j\_1\le n\_1,\,k\le r}\bigl\lvert w\_1(t,C\_1(j\_1),k)-\mathbf{w}\_1(\lfloor t/\varepsilon\rfloor,j\_1,k)\bigr\rvert$ and $d\_2(t):=\max\_{j\_2\le n\_2}\bigl\lvert w\_2(t,C\_2(j\_2))-\mathbf{w}\_2(\lfloor t/\varepsilon\rfloor,j\_2)\bigr\rvert$, one may set $\mathscr{D}\_T(W,\mathbf{W}):=\sup\_{t\le T}\max\bigl\lbrace d\_1(t),d\_2(t)\bigr\rbrace$.
