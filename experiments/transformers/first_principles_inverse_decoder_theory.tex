\section{Prompt-induced inverse problem}

Let one task be an affine operator
\[
 A(z)=A_0+\sum_{k=1}^K z_k A_k,
 \qquad T=\operatorname{span}(A_1,\ldots,A_K).
\]
A weak-form prompt gives $G\in\R^{n\times K}$ and $b\in\R^n$.  After an
orthonormal gauge has been fixed for the learned operator dictionary, the
decoder target is
\begin{equation}
 z_\star=\arg\min_z
 \frac{\tau}{2}\|Gz-b\|_2^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|z\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 H z_\star=c,
 \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
where $H=\tau G^\top G+\lambda I$, $c=\tau G^\top b$, and
$\tau,\lambda>0$.  The observable coefficient subspace is
\[
 S=\range(G^\top),\qquad r=\rank(G).
\]
This is distinct from the physical operator subspace $T$.  The encoder is
judged by recovery of $T$; the prompt-conditioned decoder head is judged by
recovery of $S$.

\begin{remark}[Gauge and regularization]
For a general change of dictionary $A\mapsto AQ$, the coordinates transform as
$z\mapsto Q^{-1}z$.  Euclidean ridge is not invariant under $GL_K$.  One must
either orthonormalize the operator dictionary, leaving only an orthogonal
ambiguity, or replace $\|z\|^2$ by $z^\top Mz$ with the covariant operator Gram
matrix $M$.  All statements below use the orthonormal gauge; the general case
follows by whitening with $M^{1/2}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Covariance under a dictionary change of basis]
Let the row-wise vectorized physical dictionary be $D\in\R^{K\times p}$,
$M=DD^\top$, and let $S\in GL_K$.  Under
\[
 D'=SD,\qquad G'=GS^\top,\qquad M'=SMS^\top,
\]
the covariantly regularized estimates
\[
 z_\star=\arg\min_z\|Gz-b\|^2+\lambda z^\top Mz,
 \qquad
 z'_\star=\arg\min_{z'}\|G'z'-b\|^2+\lambda {z'}^\top M'z'
\]
represent the same physical operator: ${z'_\star}^\top D'=z_\star^\top D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The substitution $z=S^\top z'$ makes both the data term and the quadratic
regularizer identical.  Since $S$ is invertible, it is a bijection between
the two optimization problems, and the represented physical operators agree.
\end{proof}

\section{Subspace-derived factorized preconditioner}

For an orthonormal $Q\in\R^{K\times s}$ returned by one attention head, define
\begin{equation}
 B_Q=\lambda^{-1}(I-QQ^\top)
 +Q(Q^\top H Q)^{-1}Q^\top .
 \label{eq:ritz-preconditioner}
\end{equation}
Only $Q$ is learned.  Orthonormalization, the projected matrix, and its small
Cholesky solve are fixed algebraic operations.

\begin{proposition}[Positivity and factorized cost]
$B_Q$ is SPD.  After an $O(Ks^2+s^3)$ construction, its application costs
$O(Ks+s^2)$ and does not require a dense $K\times K$ matrix.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every $x$ decomposes uniquely as $x=Qa+x_\perp$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:ritz-preconditioner} gives
\[
 x^\top B_Qx=\lambda^{-1}\|x_\perp\|^2
 +a^\top(Q^\top H Q)^{-1}a>0
\]
for $x\ne0$.  The stated cost follows by applying the two thin factors and
solving the fixed $s\times s$ Cholesky system.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact recovery gives the exact inverse]
If $s=r$ and $\range(Q)=S$, then $B_Q=H^{-1}$.
\label{thm:exact-subspace}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both $S$ and $S^\perp=\ker G$ are invariant under $H$.  On $S$, the first term
of \eqref{eq:ritz-preconditioner} vanishes and the second is the inverse of the
restriction of $H$.  On $S^\perp$, $H=\lambda I$ and $B_Q=\lambda^{-1}I$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is also the Woodbury identity in geometric form.  If
$\sqrt\tau G=U\Sigma V^\top$ is a thin SVD, then
\[
 H^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}I-
 V\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 \frac{\sigma_j^2}{\lambda(\lambda+\sigma_j^2)}
 \right)V^\top .
\]
The inverse differs from the ridge baseline by a negative rank-$r$ correction
on $S$; this sign is dictated by the inverse problem, not chosen by an
architecture search.

\begin{remark}[When decoder-side subspace learning is nontrivial]
If the exact matrix $G$ and its full rank $r$ are already given to the decoder,
then a fixed thin QR or SVD recovers $S=\range(G^\top)$; no statistical learning
is needed.  A learned decoder head is meaningful only when $s<r$, when $G$ is
noisy or encoder-generated, or when a full factorization is excluded by the
computational model.  The physical operator space $T$ remains a genuinely
learned encoder object.  Neither Heavy--Ball nor PCG identifies $T$ on its own;
the solver merely propagates the end-to-end solution loss to the encoder.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Subspace error controls the effective spectrum]
Let $V$ be an orthonormal basis of $S$, let $Q$ have $r$ columns, and set
$\varepsilon=\|QQ^\top-VV^\top\|_{\op}$.  If
$\lambda I\preceq H\preceq L_HI$, then
\[
 \|B_Q-H^{-1}\|_{\op}
 \leq C_{\lambda,L_H}\varepsilon,
 \quad
 C_{\lambda,L_H}
 =\frac{1+2\sqrt2}{\lambda}
  +\frac{2\sqrt2L_H}{\lambda^2}.
\]
Consequently, with
$\delta=L_HC_{\lambda,L_H}\varepsilon<1$, every eigenvalue of
$B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2}$ lies in $[1-\delta,1+\delta]$ and
\[
 \cond(B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2})
 \leq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}.
 \label{eq:kappa-transfer}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
After a right orthogonal alignment, the standard principal-angle relation
gives $\|Q-V\|_{\op}\leq\sqrt2\varepsilon$.  Add and subtract the aligned
Ritz terms.  Since both projected operators are bounded below by $\lambda I$,
the resolvent identity bounds the difference of their inverses by
$2L_H\|Q-V\|_{\op}/\lambda^2$.  The two outer-factor perturbations contribute
$2\|Q-V\|_{\op}/\lambda$, while the complementary projector contributes
$\varepsilon/\lambda$.  This proves the first display.  Finally,
\[
 \|H^{1/2}(B_Q-H^{-1})H^{1/2}\|_{\op}
 \leq L_H\|B_Q-H^{-1}\|_{\op},
\]
and the eigenvalue and condition-number claims follow from Weyl's inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Near-initialization trainability from neural quadratic forms}

The local training theory depends on which attention blocks are trainable.
This distinction is independent of the numerical controller.  We use the
neural quadratic form (NQF) normal forms of
\cite{ziyin2026neuralquadraticforms}, including their separate propositions
for full multi-head attention and query--key-only attention.

For one full attention head, write
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm full}(X,x)
 =o^\top V X\,\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
 \frac{X^\top K^\top Qx}{\sqrt{d_k}}
 \right).
 \label{eq:full-attention-nqf}
\end{equation}
Let $\bar x=X\mathbf 1/N$ and
$\Sigma_X=XX^\top/N-\bar x\bar x^\top$.  If all four blocks are scaled from
fixed directions as $(Q,K,V,o)\mapsto
\varepsilon(Q,K,V,o)$, direct Taylor expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm full}(\varepsilon)
 =\varepsilon^2 o^\top V\bar x
 +\frac{\varepsilon^4}{\sqrt{d_k}}
 o^\top V\Sigma_XK^\top Qx+O(\varepsilon^6).
 \label{eq:full-attention-orders}
\end{equation}
Thus value/readout learning is present in the quadratic NQF, but learned
query--key routing is not: it first affects the output at quartic order.  If a
loss residual is $O(1)$, its $V,o$ gradients are $O(\varepsilon)$ whereas its
$Q,K$ gradients are $O(\varepsilon^3)$.

For query--key-only attention with a fixed nonzero value/readout vector $c$,
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm QK}(X,x)
 =c^\top X\,\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
 \frac{X^\top K^\top Qx}{\sqrt{d_k}}
 \right),
\end{equation}
the normal form is instead
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm QK}(\varepsilon)
 =c^\top\bar x
 +\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\sqrt{d_k}}
 (\Sigma_Xc)^\top K^\top Qx+O(\varepsilon^4).
 \label{eq:qk-only-orders}
\end{equation}
Now prompt-covariance routing is quadratic and both $Q,K$ receive
$O(\varepsilon)$ residual gradients.  The statement ``query and key enter at
quartic order'' is therefore true for the all-blocks-small model
\eqref{eq:full-attention-nqf}, not for the fixed-value head used by our
decoder.

\begin{proposition}[Normal form of the decoder head before QR]
Let $v_i=g_i/\|g_i\|$ be the fixed value rows, let
$\bar v=m^{-1}\sum_i v_i$, and let
$\Sigma_v=m^{-1}\sum_i(v_i-\bar v)(v_i-\bar v)^\top$.  For slot $s$, the raw
routed row of \texttt{OneHeadObservableSubspace} satisfies, when both its key
matrix and slot query are scaled by $\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
 u_s(\varepsilon)
 =\bar v+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\sqrt{d_h}}
 \Sigma_vW_K^\top q_s+O(\varepsilon^4).
 \label{eq:decoder-qk-nqf}
\end{equation}
Hence its learned residual correction is a prompt-covariance direction at
quadratic order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The logits are
$\varepsilon^2q_s^\top W_Kv_i/\sqrt{d_h}$.  Applying the softmax Jacobian
$J(0)=I/m-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/m^2$ and summing the fixed values gives
\eqref{eq:decoder-qk-nqf}; the next softmax term is quartic in
$\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

There are two boundaries.  First, the production head initializes $W_K$ near
an identity embedding and the slot queries at nonzero scale, so it is not in
the all-zero asymptotic regime; this deliberately avoids a vanishing routing
signal.  Second, the reduced QR is not smooth at a rank-deficient zero raw
output.  Equation~\eqref{eq:decoder-qk-nqf} therefore certifies the
\emph{pre-QR routing map}, not an NQF expansion of QR, the Ritz solve, or the
complete decoder.  Those downstream maps remain fixed exact algebra.

\paragraph{Training modes are not Krylov modes.}
The NQF order parameter $M=WW^\top$ describes how parameter features switch
on over optimization time.  PCG residual modes are eigencomponents of
$B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2}$ over inference iterations.  Identifying the two would
require an additional alignment theorem.  Moreover, the generalized
Lotka--Volterra reduction in \cite{ziyin2026neuralquadraticforms} requires
commuting structure matrices (or a stated isotropic special case); generic
PDE prompts do not satisfy that hypothesis.  NQF therefore changes our
initialization and block-gradient diagnostics, but not the PCG recurrence or
its convergence proof once the prompt-fixed SPD $B_Q$ has been constructed.

\paragraph{Focused audit.}
The script
\texttt{audit\_nqf\_attention\_residual\_corrections.py} measures the local
orders in float64 on common synthetic prompts.  The fitted slopes are
$2.0000$ for the total full-MHA output, $4.0001$ for its routed part,
$3.0001$ and $1.0001$ for its Q/K and V/O loss gradients, and $2.0000$ and
$1.0000$ for the QK-only routed output and Q/K gradient.  The decoder's
pre-QR correction has slope $2.0000$, and the remainder after
\eqref{eq:decoder-qk-nqf} has slope $4.0000$.  These checks validate Taylor
orders, not end-to-end solver superiority.

\section{Three exact tied decoder cells}

The prompt HVP is the fixed linear-attention contraction
\[
 v\longmapsto Hv
 =\tau\sum_{i=1}^n g_i(g_i^\top v)+\lambda v.
\]

\paragraph{Heavy--Ball.}
With persistent state $[Z_\ell,Z_{\ell-1}]$,
\[
 r_\ell=c-HZ_\ell,
 \qquad
 Z_{\ell+1}=Z_\ell+\alpha B_Qr_\ell
 +\beta(Z_\ell-Z_{\ell-1}).
\]
There is one HVP and one factorized preconditioner application per tied
macro-block, and no division.
The coefficients may either be two shared learned scalars, constrained to a
uniform Jury-stable region, or may be constructed from a prompt-predicted
interval by the fixed formulas
\[
 \alpha(\widehat\mu,\widehat L)
 =\frac{4}{(\sqrt{\widehat L}+\sqrt{\widehat\mu})^2},\qquad
 \beta(\widehat\mu,\widehat L)
 =\left(\frac{\sqrt{\widehat L}-\sqrt{\widehat\mu}}
 {\sqrt{\widehat L}+\sqrt{\widehat\mu}}\right)^2.
\]
In the latter variant a small MLP predicts only two spectral scalars from
fixed invariant prompt reductions.  It neither represents the HVP nor
approximates vector arithmetic.  Stability only requires the actual Jury
condition
\[
 0\leq\beta<1,\qquad
 \alpha\lambda_{\max}(B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2})<2(1+\beta).
\]
Full spectral coverage is sufficient for the classical minimax rate, but is
not necessary for stability.

\paragraph{PCG.}
With persistent state $[Z,R,S,P,\rho]$ and transient token $W=HP$,
\[
 S=B_QR,\quad \rho=R^\top S,\quad
 \alpha=\rho/(P^\top W),
\]
\[
 Z^+=Z+\alpha P,\quad R^+=R-\alpha W,\quad
 S^+=B_QR^+,\quad
 \beta=(R^+)^\top S^+/\rho,\quad P^+=S^++\beta P.
\]
Scalar contractions, guarded quotients, and routing are fixed primitives.
The preconditioner is computed once from the prompt and remains fixed; if it
depends on the current residual, this is flexible CG rather than PCG.

\paragraph{Certified Heavy--Ball with PCG fallback.}
Run $L$ Heavy--Ball blocks and compute the fixed residual certificate
\[
 \eta_L=\frac{r_L^\top B_Qr_L}{c^\top B_Qc}.
\]
For a prescribed threshold $\varepsilon$, retain the division-free HB output
when $\eta_L\leq\varepsilon$ and otherwise route that task to the exact PCG
cell with the same fixed $B_Q$.  This comparison and routing are hard-coded;
there is no learned solver selector.  If
$\mu I\preceq B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2}$, then every accepted HB output obeys
\[
 \|Z_L-z_\star\|_H^2
 =r_L^\top H^{-1}r_L
 \leq \mu^{-1}r_L^\top B_Qr_L
 \leq \frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}c^\top B_Qc.
\]
Thus the certificate turns ``retain HB while it is quasi-PCG'' into a
per-prompt first-principles rule; PCG is paid only on the rejected tail.

\paragraph{Chebyshev.}
Given a certified interval $[\mu,L]$ containing the spectrum of
$B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2}$, store two additional scalar tokens.  With
\[
 d=\frac{L+\mu}{2},\qquad h=\frac{L-\mu}{2},\qquad
 \alpha_0=d^{-1},\qquad \beta_0=0,
\]
the tied block generates its next coefficients exactly:
\[
 \alpha_{\ell+1}
 =\left(d-\frac{h^2}{4}\alpha_\ell\right)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \beta_{\ell+1}=\frac{h^2}{4}\alpha_\ell\alpha_{\ell+1}.
\]
The vector update is the same routed second-order update as Heavy--Ball.  Thus
the block weights remain tied although its scalar state is nonstationary.  A
small MLP may estimate only $(\mu,L)$ from invariant prompt summaries; all
recurrence arithmetic remains fixed.  A log-center/log-width output enforces
$0<\widehat\mu<\widehat L$.  Its guarantee is conditional on the coverage
event $[\mu_{\rm true},L_{\rm true}]\subseteq
[\widehat\mu,\widehat L]$.

\paragraph{Strict loop form versus compiled schedule.}
The strict recurrent realization stores
$[Z_\ell,Z_{\ell-1},\alpha_\ell,\beta_\ell]$ and applies one tied vector block
plus the exact scalar recurrence above.  Equivalently, let
$q=h^2/4$ and let $r_\pm$ be the roots of $r^2-dr+q=0$.  If
$t=r_-/r_+$, then all layer weights can be constructed simultaneously as
\[
 \alpha_\ell=\frac{1}{r_+}
 \frac{1-t^{\ell+1}}{1-t^{\ell+2}},
 \qquad
 \beta_0=0,
 \qquad
 \beta_\ell=q\alpha_{\ell-1}\alpha_\ell.
\]
This closed form is the exact solution of the scalar-token recurrence.  It is
a compilation optimization and does not turn the MLP into an approximation of
solver arithmetic.

\paragraph{When the interval head is genuinely in-context.}
Let $s(\mathcal C)$ be fixed invariant reductions of the prompt-induced
effective operator and let
$(\widehat\mu,\widehat L)=\Phi_\psi(s(\mathcal C))$.  The meta-parameters
$\psi$ are frozen at test time, but a new prompt changes the interval and hence
the entire exact Chebyshev weight schedule.  This is in-context conditioning,
not test-time optimization.  A necessary causal audit compares the conditioned
head with (i) prompt features permuted between tasks, (ii) a prompt-independent
interval calibrated on disjoint tasks, and (iii) exact spectral endpoints.
If permutation does not hurt and the constant control is indistinguishable,
the head has not demonstrated useful ICL and must be reported only as a global
spectral calibration device.

\begin{theorem}[Exact realization and convergence]
Assume $H$ and $B_Q$ are SPD.  The three macro-blocks reproduce the
preconditioned HB, PCG, and Chebyshev recurrences exactly.  In particular,
none of their arithmetic is learned.  Let
\(\kappa=\cond(B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2})\) and
\(q=(\sqrt\kappa-1)/(\sqrt\kappa+1)\).  Then PCG satisfies
\[
 \|Z_\ell-z_\star\|_H
 \leq 2q^\ell\|Z_0-z_\star\|_H.
\]
With the classical optimal tied Heavy--Ball coefficients, every scalar
spectral recurrence has asymptotic factor at most $q$ (with the usual linear
prefactor at repeated endpoint roots).  Under exact subspace recovery,
$B_Q=H^{-1}$ and either controller with its unit correction solves in one
macro-block.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expanding each routed state update gives the named numerical method exactly.
The PCG estimate is the standard Chebyshev polynomial bound on the
preconditioned spectrum.  Diagonalizing
$B_Q^{1/2}HB_Q^{1/2}$ reduces Heavy--Ball to independent second-order scalar
recurrences; the optimal parameters place all characteristic roots in the
disk of radius $q$.  The final statement follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-subspace}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Controller hierarchy for a common preconditioner]
Fix $B_Q$, the initialization, and an HVP budget $\ell$.  In exact arithmetic,
the PCG iterate minimizes the $H$-norm error over the corresponding
preconditioned Krylov affine space.  Every Heavy--Ball or Chebyshev iterate at
the same depth belongs to this space.  Consequently,
\[
 \|e_\ell^{\rm PCG}\|_H
 \leq \|e_\ell^{\rm Cheb}\|_H,
 \qquad
 \|e_\ell^{\rm PCG}\|_H
 \leq \|e_\ell^{\rm HB}\|_H.
\]
For a certified interval, Chebyshev is the minimax predetermined polynomial
method on that interval.  Heavy--Ball instead has constant tied coefficients
and the smallest division-free state; Richardson is its special case
$\beta=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both second-order iterations produce a polynomial in the preconditioned
operator applied to the initial residual and hence lie in the same Krylov
affine space.  The inequalities follow from the Galerkin optimality of PCG.
The remaining claim is the minimax property of shifted and scaled Chebyshev
polynomials.
\end{proof}

Heavy--Ball therefore cannot dominate exact PCG in error per HVP when both use
the same learned preconditioner.  It is selected as the main architecture when
its error is practically equivalent while its macro-block is cheaper.  Our
pre-registered rule is to retain Heavy--Ball when its \emph{end-to-end} risk is
at most $10\%$ above PCG (or their confidence intervals overlap) and its
implemented solve time is lower.  A ratio of decoder-only errors is not used
when PCG reaches numerical zero.  In that regime, the admissible HB solver
error must instead be negligible relative to a separately measured
encoder/statistical error floor.  Learned preconditioners are crossed between
controllers to prevent a solver-specific head from invalidating this
comparison.

The certified hybrid strengthens this population-level selection rule.  It
keeps HB on prompts whose measured final residual is below tolerance and
inherits the PCG output on every rejected prompt.  Its cost is one final HVP
for certification plus the empirical fallback rate times the PCG cost; this
rate, rather than a claim that HB dominates PCG per HVP, must be reported.
On parallel hardware this arithmetic count is not a latency guarantee:
mask compaction, synchronization, and a small irregular PCG launch must also
be measured.  An alternative comparison may therefore fix wall-clock latency
rather than HVP count, while reporting both depths explicitly.

A division-free global-HB variant learns only $(\alpha,\beta)$ with the
encoder and spectral preconditioner frozen.  A tail-aware objective such as
mean error plus CVaR, together with a uniform Jury constraint, changes no
solver algebra and requires no spectral MLP at inference.  Its admissibility
is still decided by the same end-to-end non-inferiority rule on independent
prompts, not by the training objective itself.

Combining this theorem with \eqref{eq:kappa-transfer} gives the desired causal
chain
\begin{align*}
 \text{prompt subspace error}
 &\longrightarrow \text{preconditioner error}
 \longrightarrow \text{effective condition number}\\
 &\longrightarrow \text{finite-depth decoder error}.
\end{align*}

\section{Primal--dual equivalence}

The exact identity
\[
 (\tau G^\top G+\lambda I)^{-1}\tau G^\top
 =G^\top(\tau GG^\top+\lambda I)^{-1}\tau
\]
shows that the primal and dual decoders produce the same ridge estimator.  If
$n<K$, the dual state is smaller and lives on prompt tokens; if $K\leq n$, the
primal state is smaller.  The architecture therefore chooses the smaller
active side rather than imposing the primal formulation.  On the dual side,
the learned directions are constrained to $\range(G)$; on the primal side,
they are constrained to $\range(G^\top)$.

In the PDE regime of interest, $n=mR>K$.  The primal is therefore the cheaper
state space.  A dual interpretation can nevertheless be retained without an
$n$-dimensional state: take a thin QR factorization $G=UR$ and solve
\[
 (\tau RR^\top+\lambda I)\gamma=\tau U^\top b,
 \qquad z=R^\top\gamma.
\]
This compressed dual has dimension $\min(n,K)=K$ here and is exactly equivalent
to the primal.  It is useful as a token-space interpretation; the primal
implementation avoids the additional QR when wall-clock cost is primary.

\section{Claim boundary}

This is a recurrent attention machine with one learned routing head and fixed
linear-attention contractions.  The base variant has no MLP; the adaptive
variant permits a small head that predicts only two spectral endpoints and
never emulates solver arithmetic.  The PCG quotients and the certified
fallback mask are exact fixed primitives; they are not claimed to arise from
a vanilla softmax Transformer.
The method cannot improve upon an unlimited exact solver that receives the
true $G$.  Its legitimate target is lower error at fixed depth/HVP budget by
amortizing subspace recovery across tasks while remaining in-context on each
new prompt.
