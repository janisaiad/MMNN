\section{Setup and the correct error decomposition}

One raw PDE prompt is encoded into weak equations $(\widehat G,\widehat b)$.
The ideal weak system is $(G,b)$.  Put
\[
 H=\tau G^\top G+\lambda I,
 \quad c=\tau G^\top b,
 \quad z^\star=H^{-1}c,
\]
and define $\widehat H,\widehat c,\widehat z^\star$ analogously.  Here
$\tau,\lambda>0$.  A depth-$L$ controller returns $z_L$ for the encoded
system.  The first-principles decomposition is
\begin{equation}
 z_L-z^\star
 =\underbrace{z_L-\widehat z^\star}_{\text{finite-depth solver}}
 +\underbrace{\widehat z^\star-z^\star}_{\text{encoder/system perturbation}}.
 \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
The physical reconstruction error from the learned operator dictionary is a
third, downstream term.  It must not be conflated with either term in
\eqref{eq:decomposition}.

\begin{lemma}[Exact weak-system perturbation bound]
\label{lem:ridge-perturbation}
Let $\Delta G=\widehat G-G$ and $\Delta b=\widehat b-b$.  Then
\begin{align}
 \|\widehat H-H\|_{\op}
 &\leq \tau(\|G\|_{\op}+\|\widehat G\|_{\op})
 \|\Delta G\|_{\op},\label{eq:delta-H}\\
 \|\widehat c-c\|_2
 &\leq\tau\bigl(
 \|\widehat G\|_{\op}\|\Delta b\|_2
 +\|\Delta G\|_{\op}\|b\|_2\bigr),\label{eq:delta-c}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat z^\star-z^\star\|_2
 \leq \lambda^{-1}\|\widehat c-c\|_2
 +\lambda^{-2}\|\widehat H-H\|_{\op}\|c\|_2.
 \label{eq:ridge-transfer}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand
$\widehat G^\top\widehat G-G^\top G
=\widehat G^\top\Delta G+\Delta G^\top G$ and
$\widehat G^\top\widehat b-G^\top b
=\widehat G^\top\Delta b+\Delta G^\top b$.
For the last display use
\[
 \widehat H^{-1}\widehat c-H^{-1}c
 =\widehat H^{-1}(\widehat c-c)
 -\widehat H^{-1}(\widehat H-H)H^{-1}c
\]
and $H,\widehat H\succeq\lambda I$.
\end{proof}

This lemma is the missing bridge from encoder generalization to any of the
three controllers.  It uses the actual weak-feature errors rather than an
unjustified independence approximation between $G$ and the encoder residual.

\section{One learned geometry, three optimization terms}

Let $B_\theta(\mathcal C)\succ0$ be computed once from the encoded prompt and
kept fixed through depth.  Suppose
\[
 \sigma(B_\theta^{1/2}\widehat H B_\theta^{1/2})
 \subseteq[\mu,M],
 \qquad \kappa=M/\mu.
\]
All bounds below are conditional on the encoded prompt and can subsequently be
averaged over tasks.

\subsection{Heavy--Ball}

For the optimal constant coefficients, define
\[
 q=\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1},
 \qquad C_L(q)=1+L(1+q).
\]
With $z_{-1}=z_0=0$, the exact error polynomial obeys
\[
 p_{-1}^{\rm HB}=p_0^{\rm HB}=1,
 \quad
 p_{\ell+1}^{\rm HB}(x)
 =(1+\beta-\alpha x)p_\ell^{\rm HB}(x)
 -\beta p_{\ell-1}^{\rm HB}(x),
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 \|z_L^{\rm HB}-\widehat z^\star\|_{B_\theta^{-1}}
 \leq C_L(q)q^L
 \|\widehat z^\star\|_{B_\theta^{-1}}.
 \label{eq:hb-envelope}
\end{equation}
The polynomial is independent of the right-hand side.  Conditional Gaussian
trace formulas therefore remain exact.

For prompt-adaptive HB, let
$[\widehat\mu,\widehat M]$ be predicted from $\widehat H$ and the recovered
subspace but not from $\widehat b$, and substitute it into the analytic
minimax formulas for $(\alpha,\beta)$.  Define the actual stability event
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm Jury}^{\rm HB}
 =\{0\leq\beta<1,\ \alpha M<2(1+\beta)\}.
\]
On this event the scalar recurrence is stable on the true spectrum.  Full
coverage $[\mu,M]\subseteq[\widehat\mu,\widehat M]$ is a stronger sufficient
condition giving equation~\eqref{eq:hb-envelope} with the predicted interval,
but it is not necessary for stability.  The resulting polynomial remains
independent of $\widehat b$, so the conditional trace calculation is
unchanged.  Across tasks, the spectral MLP and an additive failure term
proportional to $\Pr((\mathcal E_{\rm Jury}^{\rm HB})^c)$ enter the population
bound.

\subsection{Chebyshev}

Let $[\widehat\mu,\widehat M]$ be produced from $\widehat H$ and the recovered
subspace, but not from $\widehat b$.  On the coverage event
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm cov}
 =\{[\mu,M]\subseteq[\widehat\mu,\widehat M]\},
\]
the fixed scalar recurrence generates a polynomial
$p_L^{\rm Ch}(x)$ independent of the right-hand side and gives
\begin{equation}
 \|z_L^{\rm Ch}-\widehat z^\star\|_{B_\theta^{-1}}
 \leq 2q_{\rm Ch}^{L}
 \|\widehat z^\star\|_{B_\theta^{-1}},
 \quad
 q_{\rm Ch}
 =\frac{\sqrt{\widehat M/\widehat\mu}-1}
 {\sqrt{\widehat M/\widehat\mu}+1}.
 \label{eq:cheb-envelope}
\end{equation}
Outside $\mathcal E_{\rm cov}$ there is no convergence guarantee.  A valid
population-risk statement must therefore include either a deterministic
certificate or a sensitivity-weighted failure term.  More precisely, if
$S(\mathcal C)$ is any taskwise envelope for the squared physical error when
coverage fails, then the uncontrolled contribution is
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak F_{\rm cov}
 =\E\!\left[S(\mathcal C)\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{\rm cov}^{c}}\right],
 \label{eq:sensitivity-coverage-failure}
\end{equation}
not merely a constant times $\Pr(\mathcal E_{\rm cov}^{c})$ unless $S$ is
uniformly bounded.  This distinction is necessary because a prompt-independent
interval can attain high marginal coverage while missing a small set of
spectrally sensitive tasks.  Reporting only interval-regression MSE or marginal
coverage is therefore insufficient.

The scalar recurrence is also a strict tied loop.  If
$z_{-1}=z_0=0$, its state is
$[z_\ell,z_{\ell-1},\alpha_\ell,\beta_\ell]$, and the next block performs the
exact hard-coded update
\[
 z_{\ell+1}=z_\ell+\alpha_\ell
 (\widehat b-\widehat H z_\ell)
 +\beta_\ell(z_\ell-z_{\ell-1}).
\]
For fixed predicted endpoints, the scalar coefficient state has a closed-form
solution and may equivalently be compiled once into a length-$L$ schedule.
This compilation changes neither the polynomial nor the hypothesis class; it
does not turn the controller into a non-looping architecture.

To call the interval head an in-context controller rather than a global
calibration artefact, let $\xi(\mathcal C)$ be its prompt-derived features and
$h_\phi(\xi)=(\widehat\mu,\widehat M)$.  At test time $\phi$ is fixed and only
$\xi$ changes.  A necessary causal audit compares
\begin{equation}
 h_\phi(\xi(\mathcal C)),\qquad
 h_\phi(\xi(\pi(\mathcal C))),\qquad
 (\overline\mu,\overline M),
 \label{eq:icl-causal-audit}
\end{equation}
on identical held-out tasks, where $\pi$ is an independent task permutation
and $(\overline\mu,\overline M)$ is calibrated on disjoint tasks.  A material
loss of solver accuracy under permutation, beyond any change in aggregate
coverage, proves that the frozen head uses task-specific prompt information.
It does not by itself prove optimality, but it rules out the claim that the
gain is solely due to a learned global interval.

\subsection{PCG}

Fixed-preconditioner PCG satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|z_L^{\rm PCG}-\widehat z^\star\|_{\widehat H}
 \leq 2q^L\|\widehat z^\star\|_{\widehat H}.
 \label{eq:pcg-envelope}
\end{equation}
It also minimizes the energy error over the depth-$L$ preconditioned Krylov
space.  Unlike the other two controllers, its realized polynomial depends on
$\widehat b$ through the adaptive inner products.  Thus the uniform envelope
transfers, but a fixed-polynomial Gaussian trace identity does not.

\begin{theorem}[Common encoder--decoder transfer]
\label{thm:common-transfer}
Let $\varepsilon_L(\mathcal C)$ denote the appropriate conditional solver
envelope from \eqref{eq:hb-envelope}, \eqref{eq:cheb-envelope}, or
\eqref{eq:pcg-envelope}, converted to Euclidean norm.  Then
\begin{align}
 \E\|z_L-z^\star\|_2^2
 \leq{}&2\E\bigl[\varepsilon_L(\mathcal C)^2
 \|\widehat z^\star\|_2^2\bigr]\notag\\
 &+4\lambda^{-2}\E\|\widehat c-c\|_2^2
 +4\lambda^{-4}\E\bigl[
 \|\widehat H-H\|_{\op}^2\|c\|_2^2\bigr].
 \label{eq:common-transfer}
\end{align}
For Chebyshev the first expectation is restricted to
$\mathcal E_{\rm cov}$, plus the sensitivity-weighted term
$\mathfrak F_{\rm cov}$ from
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-coverage-failure} after composition with the physical
decoder.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply $(a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2$ to
\eqref{eq:decomposition}, insert Lemma~\ref{lem:ridge-perturbation}, and apply
the corresponding conditional solver bound.
\end{proof}

The same proof composes with a Lipschitz physical map
$z\mapsto A(z)\mapsto u_\star$ and with the operator-subspace projector error.
That last composition must be performed in a fixed orthonormal gauge or with a
covariant coefficient metric.

\section{Across-task generalization complexity}

Let $N$ be the number of independent training tasks.  The learned hypothesis
class contains the encoder and the common subspace/preconditioner head.
Controller-dependent additions are as follows.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.13\linewidth}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.28\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}}
\toprule
Controller & Additional learned class & Required stability event & Trace linear in $b$\\
\midrule
HB & shared scalars or spectral-interval MLP & Jury margin or interval coverage & yes, if bounds ignore $b$\\
Chebyshev & spectral-interval MLP & interval coverage with slack & yes, if bounds ignore $b$\\
Moment Chebyshev & node--mass--upper MLP & positive Gram margin and upper coverage & yes, if head ignores $b$\\
Ritz--moment Chebyshev & none beyond routed probes & block-Krylov rank and certified upper bound & yes\\
PCG & none & positive denominators or converged mask & no\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For tied-scalar HB, compact stable parameters add only two coordinates to a
covering argument.  Routed Ritz--moment Chebyshev adds no learned hypothesis
class beyond the common head; its \(q\) HVPs and small eigensolve affect
computation and stability, not covering dimension.  Adaptive HB, Chebyshev,
and moment Chebyshev instead add
the controller MLP's actual norm-controlled parameter complexity.  The
moment controller additionally requires the regularized Gram matrix to stay
uniformly positive definite.  For adaptive HB the Lipschitz
constant grows as the Jury margin shrinks; for Chebyshev it grows as the
coverage slack shrinks.  For PCG, the solver adds no trainable parameter, but a pathwise
Lipschitz proof requires denominators bounded away from zero until the fixed
convergence mask activates.  The numerical mask is therefore part of the
mathematical architecture, not an implementation footnote.

Under bounded prompts, bounded network norms, a uniform stability margin, and
a Lipschitz truncated loss, standard parameter-covering or Rademacher arguments
retain an $N^{-1/2}$ leading rate.  The constants and entropy terms differ and
must not be hidden inside the old one-parameter Richardson bound.

For the Chebyshev interval head, this across-task generalization statement is
about the frozen map $h_\phi:\xi(\mathcal C)\mapsto
(\widehat\mu,\widehat M)$, not about approximating multiplication, division,
or the matrix--vector recurrence with an MLP.  Those algebraic operations are
exact architectural primitives.  The learned class contains only the
low-dimensional task-to-interval policy.  Its risk must combine the covered
solver term and $\mathfrak F_{\rm cov}$; an $N^{-1/2}$ bound for marginal
coverage alone does not control the latter without a bound on task
sensitivity or a sensitivity-weighted calibration loss.

\section{Joint predictive theory for controller and hyperparameters}
\label{sec:joint-predictive}

The preceding envelopes certify a controller chosen in advance.  They do not
yet predict which controller, depth, or coefficients minimize the physical
risk.  We now give an exact finite-dimensional objective before taking any
replica limit.

For one encoded task, write
\[
 A_\theta=B_\theta^{1/2}\widehat H B_\theta^{1/2},
 \qquad d_\theta=B_\theta^{1/2}\widehat c,
 \qquad y^\star=A_\theta^{-1}d_\theta,
 \qquad \widehat z^\star=B_\theta^{1/2}y^\star.
\]
Let $p_{s,L}(\,\cdot\,;\eta)$ be the exact residual polynomial for controller
$s\in\{\mathrm{HB},\mathrm{Ch},\mathrm{R}\}$, depth $L$, and scalar
hyperparameters $\eta$.  Starting from zero gives
\[
 z_{s,L}-\widehat z^\star
 =-B_\theta^{1/2}p_{s,L}(A_\theta;\eta)y^\star.
\]
Let $F_{\mathcal C}(z)$ be the physical query decoder, put
$r_{\mathcal C}=F_{\mathcal C}(\widehat z^\star)-u_\star$, and denote its
Jacobian at $\widehat z^\star$ by $J_{\mathcal C}$.  If
$A_\theta=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^\top$, define
\[
 v=U^\top y^\star,
 \qquad
 C=U^\top B_\theta^{1/2}J_{\mathcal C}^\top J_{\mathcal C}
 B_\theta^{1/2}U.
\]
Two taskwise signed spectral measures contain all quantities needed by the
linearized physical risk:
\begin{align*}
 \Gamma_{\mathcal C}
 &=\sum_{i,j}v_i v_j C_{ij}\,
 \delta_{(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)},\\
 \zeta_{\mathcal C}
 &=\sum_i v_i
 \left\langle r_{\mathcal C},
 J_{\mathcal C}B_\theta^{1/2}Ue_i\right\rangle
 \delta_{\lambda_i}.
\end{align*}
Although $\Gamma_{\mathcal C}$ need not be a positive measure, its quadratic
form is nonnegative for every real polynomial.  No commutation between the
physical Jacobian and $A_\theta$ has been assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Exact joint polynomial risk]
\label{thm:joint-polynomial-risk}
If $F_{\mathcal C}$ is affine in $z$, the population physical risk of any
right-hand-side-independent polynomial controller is exactly
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_{s,L}(\theta,\eta)
 ={}&\mathcal R_{\rm enc}(\theta)
 -2\int p_{s,L}(\lambda;\eta)\,
 d\overline\zeta_\theta(\lambda)\notag\\
 &+\iint p_{s,L}(\lambda;\eta)
 p_{s,L}(\lambda';\eta)\,
 d\overline\Gamma_\theta(\lambda,\lambda'),
 \label{eq:exact-joint-risk}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal R_{\rm enc}=\E\|r_{\mathcal C}\|^2$ and the bars denote
expectation over tasks.  If $F_{\mathcal C}$ has a locally Lipschitz Jacobian,
the same display is the risk of its first-order physical linearization and
the omitted remainder is
$O(\E[\|r_{\mathcal C}\|\|e_{s,L}\|^2]
+\E\|e_{s,L}\|^3+\E\|e_{s,L}\|^4)$ under bounded moments, where
$e_{s,L}=z_{s,L}-\widehat z^\star$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an affine decoder, insert
$F_{\mathcal C}(z_{s,L})-u_\star
=r_{\mathcal C}-J_{\mathcal C}B_\theta^{1/2}
p_{s,L}(A_\theta)y^\star$ and expand its squared norm in the eigenbasis of
$A_\theta$.  The nonlinear statement follows from Taylor's theorem and
Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

The predictive, latency-aware choice is consequently
\begin{equation}
 (s^\star,L^\star,\eta^\star,\theta^\star)
 \in\operatorname*{argmin}_{s,L,\eta,\theta}
 \left\{\mathcal R_{s,L}(\theta,\eta)
 +\tau_{\rm hw}T_s(L,\theta)\right\},
 \label{eq:joint-hyperparameter-predictor}
\end{equation}
subject to the controller's deterministic stability constraints.  Here
$T_s$ is a measured or analytic hardware cost and $\tau_{\rm hw}$ selects a
point on the risk--latency Pareto frontier.  Equation
\eqref{eq:joint-hyperparameter-predictor} predicts the preconditioner through
$\theta$, the controller, its depth, and its scalar hyperparameters jointly;
it is not a post-hoc convergence bound.

\begin{theorem}[risk-optimal fixed polynomial from spectral moments]
\label{thm:risk-optimal-moment-polynomial}
Suppose the conditional or population \(H\)-energy solver risk has the form
\[
 \mathcal E_L(p)=\int p(\lambda)^2\,\gamma(d\lambda),
 \qquad p(0)=1,\qquad \deg p\leq L,
\]
for a finite positive spectral measure \(\gamma\) supported in
\((0,\overline L]\).  Let \(m_k=\int\lambda^k\,\gamma(d\lambda)\) and assume
the Hankel matrix
\[
 \mathsf H_L=(m_{i+j})_{i,j=1}^L
\]
is nonsingular.  The unique risk-optimal right-hand-side-independent
polynomial is
\[
 p_L^\star(\lambda)=1+\sum_{j=1}^Lc_j^\star\lambda^j,
 \qquad
 \mathsf H_Lc^\star=-(m_1,\ldots,m_L)^\top .
 \label{eq:moment-optimal-polynomial}
\]
It weakly dominates every HB or Chebyshev polynomial of the same degree for
this measure.  It need not be minimax on the support and can be arbitrarily
better when \(\gamma\) is concentrated on a small number of spectral
clusters.  Instancewise PCG with the same preconditioner still weakly
dominates it because PCG adapts its polynomial to the realized right-hand
side.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(p=1+\sum_{j=1}^Lc_j\lambda^j\).  Expanding the integral gives
\[
 \mathcal E_L(p)
 =m_0+2c^\top(m_1,\ldots,m_L)^\top+c^\top\mathsf H_Lc.
\]
The displayed linear system is the first-order condition of this strictly
convex quadratic.  HB and Chebyshev are feasible fixed polynomials, while
the final claim is the Galerkin optimality of PCG in the realized Krylov
space.
\end{proof}

This theorem gives a first-principles role to a small MLP without asking it to
approximate solver arithmetic.  The prompt head may estimate invariant
cluster nodes and masses, or equivalently the moments
\((m_0,\ldots,m_{2L})\).  The Hankel solve in
\eqref{eq:moment-optimal-polynomial}, conversion of
\((1-p_L^\star(\lambda))/\lambda\), and a fixed Horner or Clenshaw evaluator
are hard-coded exact algebra.  An endpoint-only Chebyshev MLP is the
support-only special case and necessarily discards cluster multiplicities.
For numerical stability the implementation should solve the equivalent Gram
system in a shifted Chebyshev basis rather than form high monomial powers.
The spectral nodes need not include the stability endpoint: the learned
positive measure and the interval on which Clenshaw is evaluated are distinct
objects.  In the implementation the MLP therefore predicts ordered positive
nodes, positive normalized masses, and a positive expansion of a Ritz scale;
the expansion is capped by the matrix-free global upper certificate.  The
small Gram solve is performed in float64 and only its coefficients are cast
back to the vector-loop precision.
The same construction applies to the affine physical risk in
\eqref{eq:exact-joint-risk}, where the averaged bivariate measure supplies a
general positive-semidefinite Gram matrix instead of the scalar Hankel
matrix.

There is an unavoidable stability boundary.  If the Clenshaw interval is
$[0,\widehat L]$, its shifted coordinate is
$x(\lambda)=2\lambda/\widehat L-1$.  For a missed eigenvalue
$\lambda>\widehat L$,
\[
 T_j(x(\lambda))
 =\cosh\!\left(j\operatorname{arcosh}x(\lambda)\right),
\]
so a rare upper-endpoint miss can grow exponentially with degree and dominate
an arithmetic-mean risk.  Consequently, high average validation coverage is
not a certificate: either $\widehat L$ must be a genuine upper bound, or an
explicit residual guard must handle misses.  This conclusion uses only the
exact Chebyshev identity and does not depend on a neural approximation
argument.

For HB, $\eta=(\alpha,\beta)$ and the feasible set is
\[
 0\leq\beta<1,
 \qquad
 \alpha\lambda_+(\theta)<2(1+\beta).
\]
Given the two averaged measures, the distribution-aware optimum is only a
two-dimensional deterministic minimization.  Its gradients require no
learned arithmetic: differentiate the exact recurrence according to
\begin{align*}
 \partial_\alpha p_{\ell+1}
 &=-\lambda p_\ell+(1+\beta-\alpha\lambda)
 \partial_\alpha p_\ell-\beta\partial_\alpha p_{\ell-1},\\
 \partial_\beta p_{\ell+1}
 &=p_\ell-p_{\ell-1}+(1+\beta-\alpha\lambda)
 \partial_\beta p_\ell-\beta\partial_\beta p_{\ell-1}.
\end{align*}
If only a support certificate $[\mu,M]$ is known, the distribution-free
minimax prediction is the closed form
\[
 \alpha_{\rm mm}=\frac{4}{(\sqrt M+\sqrt\mu)^2},
 \qquad
 \beta_{\rm mm}=
 \left(\frac{\sqrt M-\sqrt\mu}{\sqrt M+\sqrt\mu}\right)^2.
\]
Thus the closed-form theory and the distribution-aware theory are two levels
of the same predictor, not competing heuristics.

For a prompt-conditioned Chebyshev controller, the Bayes-optimal interval
policy is likewise defined without reference to a neural network:
\begin{equation}
 h^\star(\xi)
 \in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\widehat\mu,\widehat M}
 \E\!\left[\mathcal R_{\mathrm{Ch},L}
 (\widehat\mu,\widehat M)\mid\xi\right],
 \label{eq:bayes-chebyshev-policy}
\end{equation}
including the sensitivity-weighted failure cost from
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-coverage-failure}.  The small MLP is merely a
norm-controlled function class used to estimate $h^\star$ from finitely many
tasks.  Training it on endpoint MSE alone targets a different and generally
suboptimal object.

The assumptions used so far are only: SPD encoded normal operators, a
preconditioner and scalar policy fixed before seeing the query right-hand
side, differentiability of the physical decoder for the local nonlinear
statement, and the displayed moments.  Gaussianity, simultaneous
diagonalization, isotropy, and a large-system limit are not required.  If the
physical metric is the solver energy itself, $C$ is diagonal in the spectral
basis and $\Gamma$ reduces to a positive one-dimensional measure.

\subsection{What joint training can and cannot change}

Let $\mathcal L_L(\vartheta)$ be the training loss through a depth-$L$
polynomial decoder and $\mathcal L_\infty(\vartheta)$ the same loss through
the exact encoded solve, where $\vartheta$ denotes encoder and preconditioner
parameters.  On a compact uniformly stable parameter set, define
\[
 \delta_L=\sup_{\vartheta}\left(
 \|p_L(A_\vartheta)\|_{\op}
 +\|D_Ap_L(A_\vartheta)\|_{\op\to\op}\right).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Gradient fidelity of a deep fixed polynomial]
\label{prop:gradient-fidelity}
If the weak encoder, preconditioner, and physical loss have uniformly bounded
first derivatives on that set, then for a constant $K$ independent of $L$,
\[
 \|\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal L_L
 -\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal L_\infty\|
 \leq K\delta_L.
\]
For stable minimax HB or Chebyshev, $\delta_L$ is a polynomial factor in $L$
and $1/\mu$ times $q^L$; for optimal Richardson its exponential factor is
$(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$ instead of
$(\sqrt\kappa-1)/(\sqrt\kappa+1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the exact identity
$z_L-\widehat z^\star=-B_\theta^{1/2}p_L(A_\theta)y^\star$ and apply the chain
rule.  Uniform derivative bounds control every factor except the polynomial
and its Fr\'echet derivative, which are precisely the two terms in
$\delta_L$.  The rates follow from the corresponding stable polynomial
recurrences and their differentiated recurrences.
\end{proof}

This proposition predicts that sufficiently deep HB and Richardson can learn
the same identifiable operator subspace even though HB reaches a prescribed
gradient fidelity at a smaller asymptotic depth.  The controller advantage
appears when depth or latency is binding, or on spectral tails.  It should not
be misreported as a different identifiability theorem for the encoder.

\subsection{An equivariant one-head closure with no eigenvector order parameter}

The coordinate-token Ritz head used in the first experiments is not invariant
under an orthogonal change of coefficient dictionary.  Its replica closure
therefore needs coordinate/eigenvector overlaps in addition to eigenvalues.
The following alternative removes that hidden assumption.  All Ritz algebra
is exact; one softmax head learns only how to allocate correction mass among
the spectral tokens.

Let $\widehat H=V\operatorname{diag}(h_1,\ldots,h_K)V^\top\succ0$, fix a certified
$\overline L>1$, and set
\[
 s_H=h_{\max}/\overline L,
 \qquad \lambda_i=h_i/s_H\in(0,\overline L].
\]
Let $x_i=x(\lambda_i;\nu_K)$ contain only permutation-invariant spectral
features, where $\nu_K=K^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{\lambda_i}$.  For slot $r$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \omega_{ri}
 &=\frac{\exp\{q_r^\top k_\theta(x_i)/\sqrt{d_h}\}}
 {\sum_j\exp\{q_r^\top k_\theta(x_j)/\sqrt{d_h}\}},\\
 g_i&=1-\prod_{r=1}^{S}(1-\omega_{ri}),\\
 B_\theta(\widehat H)
 &=V\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 \frac{1-g_i}{s_H}+\frac{g_i}{h_i}
 \right)V^\top .
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:equivariant-ritz-head}
\end{equation}
There is no MLP and no learned multiplication, reciprocal, eigensolver, or
recurrence in this definition.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-$K$ spectral closure]
\label{thm:equivariant-ritz-closure}
The map in \eqref{eq:equivariant-ritz-head} is SPD and orthogonally covariant:
for every orthogonal $Q$,
\[
 B_\theta(Q^\top\widehat H Q)=Q^\top B_\theta(\widehat H)Q.
\]
It is independent of the eigenbasis selected inside repeated eigenspaces.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A_\theta
 &=B_\theta^{1/2}\widehat H B_\theta^{1/2}
 =V\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_K)V^\top,\\
 a_i&=(1-g_i)\lambda_i+g_i,
 \qquad 0<a_i\leq\overline L,
 \qquad \sum_i g_i\leq S.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:effective-pushforward-finite}
\end{equation}
Hence the effective spectral law is known exactly from the raw eigenvalue law
and the one-head gates; no eigenvector replica order parameter is required.
In particular the HB Jury constraint holds for every prompt whenever
$\alpha\overline L<2(1+\beta)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equal eigenvalues have equal invariant features and therefore equal gates, so
the spectral functional is well defined on the corresponding eigenspace and
is orthogonally covariant.  Multiplication of the commuting eigenvalue factors
gives $a_i$.  Since $g_i\in[0,1]$, $a_i$ lies between $\lambda_i$ and $1$.
Finally, the union bound gives
$g_i\leq\sum_r\omega_{ri}$ and summing over $i$ gives
$\sum_i g_i\leq S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[exact-spectrum Chebyshev needs no interval MLP]
\label{cor:exact-head-chebyshev}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:equivariant-ritz-closure}, the endpoints
\[
    \mu_{\mathcal C}=\min_i a_i,
    \qquad
    L_{\mathcal C}=\max_i a_i
\]
are already produced by the exact Ritz algebra of the preconditioner head.
Feeding these two scalars to the hard-coded Chebyshev coefficient recurrence
gives the exact promptwise Chebyshev controller that is minimax on the
enclosing interval $[\mu_{\mathcal C},L_{\mathcal C}]$, without an interval
MLP and without an additional eigendecomposition.  This does not claim
minimaxity on the finite discrete spectrum itself.  A learned interval head is
needed only when the spectral decomposition is not materialized, or when its
cost is deliberately amortized at larger coefficient dimension.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[a global covariance loses all directions under RRS]
\label{prop:rrs-global-covariance}
Let $H\succ0$ be any random prompt normal with
$\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}H<\infty$, and let $Q$ be an independent Haar
orthogonal matrix.  Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{H,Q}[Q^\top H Q]
 =\frac{\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}H}{K}I.
\]
Consequently the inverse-population-covariance preconditioner is a scalar
multiple of the identity and cannot retain a preferred eigenspace.  In
contrast, every promptwise orthogonally covariant head satisfies
\[
 B_\theta(Q^\top H Q)=Q^\top B_\theta(H)Q,
\]
so $B_\theta(Q^\top H Q)^{1/2}(Q^\top H Q)
B_\theta(Q^\top H Q)^{1/2}$ has the same eigenvalues as
$B_\theta(H)^{1/2}HB_\theta(H)^{1/2}$.  Thus its directional correction
survives arbitrary contextwise rotations without storing their orientation
in the weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Haar invariance makes $\mathbb E_Q[Q^\top H Q]$ commute with every orthogonal
matrix, hence it is scalar; trace preservation fixes the scalar.  The second
claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:equivariant-ritz-closure}, and orthogonal similarity
preserves the effective spectrum.
\end{proof}

This statement needs neither Gaussianity nor a deterministic limiting
spectrum.  It also exposes a stability distinction hidden by fixed-spectrum
models.  If the largest eigenvalue of $H$ has unbounded support, then for any
fixed scalar preconditioner, $\alpha>0$, and $\beta<1$, the HB Jury inequality
fails with positive probability.  No nonzero globally fixed step can be
uniformly certified without a spectral-tail assumption.  The promptwise
normalization in \eqref{eq:equivariant-ritz-head}, however, gives
$a_i\leq\overline L$ deterministically.  A finite-law risk or CVaR predictor
can still select the best global coefficients, but it must report this tail
risk rather than silently assuming bounded Gaussian quadratic forms.

\begin{proposition}[an eigendecomposition-free prompt-token Ritz head]
\label{prop:prompt-nystrom-head}
Let $G\in\mathbb R^{M\times K}$ be the weak-equation tokens,
$H=G^\top G+\lambda M\succ0$, and $\overline L>0$.  Suppose a single softmax
head forms $W_\theta\in\mathbb R^{S\times M}$ from scalar token features that
are invariant under $G\mapsto GQ$, and set
\[
 Y=G^\top W_\theta^\top,
 \qquad
 U=\operatorname{orth}\!\left[
 \left(I-\frac{H}{\operatorname{tr}H}\right)^rY
 \right],
 \qquad
 C=U^\top\frac{H}{s_H}U,
 \quad s_H=\frac{\operatorname{tr}H}{\overline L}.
\]
Define
\[
 \widetilde B=I+U(C^{-1}-I)U^\top,
 \quad
 \widetilde B^{1/2}=I+U(C^{-1/2}-I)U^\top,
 \quad
 A_0=\widetilde B^{1/2}\frac{H}{s_H}\widetilde B^{1/2},
\]
and $\tau=\|A_0\|_{\mathrm F}/\overline L$,
$B_\theta=\widetilde B/(s_H\tau)$.  Then $B_\theta\succ0$,
\[
 B_\theta(Q^\top H Q;GQ)=Q^\top B_\theta(H;G)Q,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\max}(B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2})
 \leq\overline L.
\]
The construction uses no $K\times K$ eigendecomposition: its only spectral
operation is the $S\times S$ decomposition of $C$.  Once $H$ is available,
its low-rank algebra costs
$O(MKd_h+rK^2S+KS^2+S^3)$ rather than $O(K^3)$ when $S\ll K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The trace scale gives
$0\prec H/s_H\preceq\overline L I$, so
$I-H/\operatorname{tr}H\succeq0$.  Invariant attention scores are unchanged by
$G\mapsto GQ$, while the values give $Y\mapsto Q^\top Y$; polynomial
filtering and orthogonalization therefore transform the selected projector
covariantly.  Since $C\succ0$, both displayed low-rank spectral functions are
SPD and the square-root identity follows by restricting to
$\operatorname{span}U$ and its orthogonal complement.  Finally
$\lambda_{\max}(A_0/\tau)\leq\|A_0/\tau\|_{\mathrm F}=\overline L$.
Only $C$ is diagonalized.
\end{proof}

The Frobenius certificate is deliberately assumption-free but can be looser
than the exact endpoint.  A seven-scalar invariant summary (effective trace
and Frobenius norm, projected endpoints, certified upper bound, subspace
residual, and context length) may feed an interval MLP.  The MLP then predicts
only $(\widehat\mu,\widehat L)$; the HB or Chebyshev coefficients remain exact
analytic functions of these scalars.  Stability still requires calibrated
coverage or the residual-guarded HB--PCG fallback.

\begin{proposition}[certified matrix-free high-mode deflation]
\label{prop:matrix-free-prompt-head}
Keep the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:prompt-nystrom-head}, but never
form $H$.  Compute $Hv=G^\top(Gv)+\lambda Mv$, set
$s_H=\operatorname{tr}(H)/\overline L$, and obtain
\[
 U=\operatorname{orth}\!\left[
 \left(\frac{H}{\operatorname{tr}H}\right)^rG^\top W_\theta^\top
 \right],
 \qquad
 C=U^\top\frac{H}{s_H}U
   =V_C\operatorname{diag}(c_i)V_C^\top.
\]
For $c_\star=\min_i c_i$, let
\[
 d_i=\min\{1,c_\star/c_i\},
 \quad
 D=V_C\operatorname{diag}(d_i-1)V_C^\top,
 \quad
 \widetilde B=I+UDU^\top.
\]
Writing $A=H/s_H$, define
\[
 R=AU-UC,
 \qquad
 d_\perp=\overline L-\operatorname{tr}C,
 \qquad
 \gamma=\|R(I+D)^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm F},
\]
\[
 \widehat L_{\rm post}
 =\frac{c_\star+d_\perp+
 \sqrt{(c_\star-d_\perp)^2+4\gamma^2}}{2},
 \qquad
 \zeta=\widehat L_{\rm post}/\overline L,
 \qquad
 B_\theta=\frac{\widetilde B}{s_H\zeta}.
\]
Then $B_\theta$ is SPD, orthogonally covariant and permutation invariant in
the weak tokens.  Moreover
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2})
 \leq\overline L.
\]
Neither $H$ nor $B_\theta$ is materialized.  With dense weak tokens, head
construction costs $O((r+1)MKS+MKd_h+KS^2+S^3)$ and stores
$O(MK+KS+S^2)$; every subsequent application costs $O(KS)$ in addition to
the matrix-free normal HVP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All attention features are scalar row invariants, whereas the routed values
transform as $G^\top W^\top\mapsto Q^\top G^\top W^\top$.  HVPs, QR and the
projected spectral function therefore give the covariance claim.  Since
$c_i>0$ and $d_i\in(0,1]$, the low-rank operator in brackets has eigenvalues
$d_i$ on $\operatorname{span}U$ and one on its complement, so it is SPD.
In the orthogonal decomposition
$\operatorname{span}U\oplus\operatorname{span}U^\perp$, positivity gives
\[
 A=\begin{bmatrix}C&R^\top\\R&A_\perp\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\max}(A_\perp)
 \leq\operatorname{tr}A_\perp
 =\overline L-\operatorname{tr}C=d_\perp.
\]
Because $I+D=c_\star C^{-1}$ on the selected space, the selected block of
$\widetilde B^{1/2}A\widetilde B^{1/2}$ is exactly $c_\star I$ and its cross
block has operator norm at most $\gamma$.  Its largest eigenvalue is bounded
by that of the scalar majorant
$\begin{bmatrix}c_\star&\gamma\\
\gamma&d_\perp\end{bmatrix}$, namely
$\widehat L_{\rm post}$.  Rescaling by $1/\zeta$ proves
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2})
 \leq\widehat L_{\rm post}/\zeta=\overline L.
\]
Every quantity in the sharper certificate is an $S\times S$ Ritz
quantity, the block residual already computed by the construction, or a
trace; no full spectrum is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[exact matrix-free head interface]
\label{prop:matrix-free-moment-interface}
Let \(Y_\theta\in\mathbb R^{K\times S}\) denote the covariant block routed by
the softmax head before its first QR factorization, let \(A=H/s_H\), and
assume \(A^rY_\theta\) has full column rank.  Define the three small block
moments
\[
 K_r=Y_\theta^\top A^{2r}Y_\theta,
 \qquad
 J_r=Y_\theta^\top A^{2r+1}Y_\theta,
 \qquad
 L_r=Y_\theta^\top A^{2r+2}Y_\theta .
\]
Up to an immaterial orthogonal change of slot coordinates, the matrix-free
construction in Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-prompt-head} is exactly
\begin{align}
 U&=A^rY_\theta K_r^{-1/2},
 &C&=K_r^{-1/2}J_rK_r^{-1/2},\\
 R^\top R&=K_r^{-1/2}L_rK_r^{-1/2}-C^2,
 &\zeta&=\zeta(C,R,\operatorname{tr}A),\\
 c_\star&=\lambda_{\min}(C),
 &B_\theta&=(s_H\zeta)^{-1}\left[
 I-UU^\top+c_\star UC^{-1}U^\top\right].
 \label{eq:matrix-free-moment-interface}
\end{align}
Consequently the learned object is the routed subspace \(Y_\theta\), while
the projected normal geometry, its spectral correction, the tight
certificate, and the preconditioner application are exact algebraic functions
of \((K_r,J_r,L_r,\operatorname{tr}A)\).  A finite-dimensional risk
calculation for this head closes
on these block moments together with the overlaps of \(U\) with the teacher,
the right-hand side, and the physical decoder metric.  It does not in
general close on the eigenvalue law of \(H\) alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Symmetric orthogonalization of \(A^rY_\theta\) gives the displayed \(U\).
Substitution yields \(U^\top AU=C\), while expanding
\((AU-UC)^\top(AU-UC)\) gives the displayed residual Gram.  The
implementation assigns multiplier
\(c_\star/c_i\) to a Ritz eigenvector with eigenvalue \(c_i\); functional
calculus therefore gives \(c_\star C^{-1}\) on \(\operatorname{span}U\) and
the identity on its orthogonal complement.  QR produces a different
orthonormal basis \(UO\), but the expression is unchanged after the
corresponding conjugation of \(C\).  The last claim follows because the
symmetric effective operator
\(B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2}\) need not share eigenvectors with \(H\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[routed block--Krylov spectral controller]
\label{prop:routed-krylov-measure}
Let
\[
 A_\theta=B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2}\succ0,\qquad
 \tau_\theta=\operatorname{tr}A_\theta,
\]
where \(B_\theta\) is the matrix-free head in
Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-moment-interface}.  Take the routed block
\(Q_0=U\) and suppose the block Krylov space
\[
 {\cal K}_q(A_\theta,Q_0)
 =\operatorname{span}\{Q_0,A_\theta Q_0,\ldots,
 A_\theta^{q-1}Q_0\}
\]
has dimension \(qS\leq K\).  Let \(Q\) be its fully reorthogonalized basis,
\(T_q=Q^\top A_\theta Q\), and
\(\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_{qS}\) the Ritz eigenvalues.  If \(qS<K\),
define
\[
 \bar\vartheta_\perp
 =\frac{\tau_\theta-\operatorname{tr}T_q}{K-qS}
\]
and the compressed energy measure
\[
 \widehat\nu_q
 =\frac1{\tau_\theta}\left[
   \sum_{i=1}^{qS}\vartheta_i\delta_{\vartheta_i}
   +(K-qS)\bar\vartheta_\perp
     \delta_{\bar\vartheta_\perp}\right].
 \label{eq:routed-krylov-energy-measure}
\]
For \(qS=K\), omit the last atom.  Then
\(\widehat\nu_q\) is a probability measure determined by prompt-only
matrix-free algebra.  Substituting its nodes and masses into
Theorem~\ref{thm:risk-optimal-moment-polynomial} gives the exact
\(\widehat\nu_q\)-optimal degree-\(L-1\) solution polynomial.  If \(qS=K\),
this is the exact expected \(H\)-energy-risk minimizer for an isotropic
coefficient prior.  The construction is gauge equivariant, independent of
the query right-hand side, and costs exactly \(q\) additional block HVP
rounds before the tied Clenshaw loop.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The trace is available without a dense operator:
\[
 \tau_\theta
 =\zeta^{-1}\left[
 \overline L+
 \operatorname{tr}\{(c_\star C^{-1}-I)C\}\right].
\]
Poincaré separation makes every \(\vartheta_i\) positive and
\(\operatorname{tr}T_q\leq\tau_\theta\), so the complement atom is
nonnegative and the displayed masses sum to one.  Under an orthogonal gauge
change, \(A_\theta,Q_0,Q\) transform covariantly and \(T_q\), its spectrum,
and \(\tau_\theta\) remain invariant.  The Gram solve and Clenshaw evaluation
are exact by Theorem~\ref{thm:risk-optimal-moment-polynomial}.  When \(qS=K\),
\(Q\) is square orthogonal and \(T_q\) is similar to \(A_\theta\).  For an
isotropic coefficient \(x\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\|r(A_\theta)x\|_{A_\theta}^2}
      {\mathbb E\|x\|_{A_\theta}^2}
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{A_\theta r(A_\theta)^2\}}
        {\operatorname{tr}A_\theta}
 =\int r(\lambda)^2\,\widehat\nu_q(d\lambda),
\]
which proves exactness in the full-space case.  Each new block is obtained by
one action of \(A_\theta\), hence one prompt HVP surrounded by two exact
low-rank square-root applications.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[fixed-slot bulk boundary and low-rank benefit]
\label{cor:fixed-slot-boundary}
Let
\[
 A_\theta=B_\theta^{1/2}HB_\theta^{1/2},
 \qquad A_0=H/(s_H\zeta) .
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_\theta-A_0)\leq 2S,
 \qquad
 \sup_x\left|F_{A_\theta}(x)-F_{A_0}(x)\right|\leq\frac{2S}{K}.
\]
Thus a bounded-score head with fixed \(S\) cannot change a normalized bulk
spectral risk by order one as \(K\to\infty\).  It can nevertheless change a
low-rank inverse-problem risk by order one when the teacher or physical
sensitivity has nonvanishing overlap with \(\operatorname{span}(U,HU)\).
A bulk improvement instead requires \(S/K\to\sigma>0\), attention
concentrating on a vanishing spectral set, or another structure that carries
order-one task risk in finitely many directions.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write
\(B_\theta^{1/2}=(s_H\zeta)^{-1/2}(I+UEU^\top)\) for the exact
\(S\)-dimensional spectral function \(E\).  Expanding \(A_\theta-A_0\) shows that its image lies
in \(\operatorname{span}(U,HU)\), hence its rank is at most \(2S\).  The
empirical spectral-distribution bound is the Hermitian rank inequality.  For
isotropic normalized trace losses this contribution vanishes with \(S/K\),
whereas a teacher supported on the affected subspace assigns it nonvanishing
mass.  This is precisely the low-rank-recovery regime.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where improvement over a pure solver can occur]
\label{rem:pure-solver-boundary}
For the same \(H\), \(B_\theta\), initialization, and HVP budget, exact PCG
still minimizes the \(H\)-energy error over the available Krylov space.
Neither the learned head nor the choice of HB or Chebyshev reverses that
Galerkin ordering.  The learned architecture can improve on a
\emph{head-free} iterative baseline only through a better prompt-conditioned
geometry and can improve on a dense direct solve only through setup, memory,
amortization, or a task-risk objective different from exact algebraic error.

For example, order the eigenvalues of \(A=H/s_H\) as
\(\lambda_1(A)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_K(A)\).  If \(U\) is the invariant
subspace of the \(S\) largest eigenvalues, then
\eqref{eq:matrix-free-moment-interface} maps all selected eigenvalues to the
smallest selected one.  The global condition number decreases from
\(\lambda_K(A)/\lambda_1(A)\) to
\(\lambda_{K-S+1}(A)/\lambda_1(A)\).  Outside this invariant case there is no
distribution-free dominance: the relevant comparison is the joint
teacher/physical spectral risk plus measured total latency.  This is why the
overlap order parameters in Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-slot-boundary} are part
of the theory rather than an optional diagnostic.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Paired randomly rotated low-rank audit.}
The script \texttt{audit\_rotated\_low\_rank\_controller\_learning.py}
isolates the two axes in Remark~\ref{rem:pure-solver-boundary}.  Every
$K=12$ prompt has two rank-two covariance outliers of strength $100$ with an
independent Haar orientation.  Richardson, HB and PCG start from identical
head weights, receive identical minibatches for $1000$ updates, and are
crossed with every exact cell on $4096$ fresh prompts for each of three seeds.
With no block-power refinement, HB training raises outlier-space overlap from
$0.878$ to $0.945$, reduces mean effective condition number from $320.1$ to
$115.7$, and lowers HB--4 risk from $0.148$ to $0.104$; Richardson training
changes the overlap only to $0.885$.  Thus the HB objective really can train
the routed space.  With one exact block-power step, however, every head has
overlap about $0.9998$ and condition number about $6.25$: the hard-coded
linear relation dominates the learned routing.

In this second regime, mean relative $H$-risks are $1.19\times10^{-2}$ for
HB--4, $1.97\times10^{-3}$ for Chebyshev--4, and $1.06\times10^{-4}$ for
PCG--4.  Including two block-HVP setup rounds, head+PCG--4 is $3.43$ times
better than head-free PCG--6.  Counting the three block directions as six
sequential scalar HVPs reverses the result: PCG--10 is $42.9$ times better.
This is a hardware-parallel latency benefit, not solver dominance.  The exact
weighted-polynomial prediction and realized stationary risks agree with
maximum mean absolute gap $3.36\times10^{-7}$.

The correlated PDE regression at $K=32,M=128,S=4,r=2,L=8$ isolates the
effect of the sharper certificate.  Across three seeds, trained HB risk drops
from $6.88\times10^{-1}$ under trace-only scaling to
$7.34\times10^{-5}$ under post-deflation scaling.  Head-free PCG--8 reaches
$2.19\times10^{-6}$, while the same learned head followed by PCG--8 reaches
$1.81\times10^{-8}$.  Charging the setup as twelve sequential scalar HVPs,
head-free PCG--20 reaches $4.74\times10^{-8}$, so the learned geometry still
wins by a factor $2.62$ at equal sequential work.  HB is therefore a viable
exact stationary loop after tight rescaling, but it remains $33.5$ times less
accurate than head-free PCG--8 on this law.

This construction deliberately deflates high modes rather than trying to
invert low modes: block power iteration finds the former without divisions,
and multipliers below one preserve the certificate.  The price is that a
global HB step based only on $\overline L$ can be conservative.  The interval
MLP therefore receives only matrix-free invariants of $C$, its Ritz residual,
row-norm dispersion and context length.  Exact endpoints may supervise this
MLP during pretraining but are absent at inference.  A residual guard routes
the rare interval miss to explicit PCG.  More precisely, if
$\eta=r^\top B_\theta r/(b^\top B_\theta b)$ and the effective spectrum is
contained in $[\mu,L_B]$, then
\[
 \frac{\lVert e\rVert_H^2}{\lVert x^\star\rVert_H^2}
 \leq \frac{L_B}{\mu}\eta.
\]
Thus the observed residual alone is not an a priori energy-error certificate
without a certified positive lower endpoint.  The deterministic guarantee of
the matrix-free head is the upper endpoint; the fallback is a robust routing
rule unless such a lower certificate is supplied.

The same interval head can be crossed with the fixed Chebyshev recurrence
without retraining.  In a three-seed audit with $8192$ held-out prompts per
seed and ten polynomial HVPs, learned Chebyshev lowers the arithmetic mean
solver risk by $30\%$ relative to learned HB.  With the same residual guard
and a four-step PCG fallback, the reduction is $25\%$ and the fallback rate
drops from $0.826\%$ to $0.773\%$.  This is evidence that the MLP learns a
reusable prompt spectral statistic rather than HB-specific arithmetic.
Nevertheless, paired PCG with ten HVPs has mean risk
$8.98\times10^{-12}$, compared with $2.11\times10^{-10}$ for guarded learned
Chebyshev at $10.031$ expected HVPs.  Thus this experiment validates the
Chebyshev architecture but rejects numerical superiority over PCG at equal
HVP budget.  The oracle Chebyshev mean, $5.74\times10^{-12}$, localizes the
remaining gap to rare endpoint-prediction failures rather than to the fixed
recurrence.

\begin{corollary}[amortized multiple-query complexity]
\label{cor:matrix-free-multiple-rhs}
Fix one prompt geometry $G$ and let $Q$ observation vectors share it.  After
one construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-prompt-head}, $T$ exact
HB, Chebyshev, or PCG blocks applied jointly to all right-hand sides cost
\[
 O\!\left((r+1)MKS+MKd_h+KS^2+S^3
          +TQ(MK+KS)\right)
\]
and require $O(MK+KS+KQ+S^2)$ storage.  Forming the dense normal matrix,
factorizing it, and applying the factor to the same queries costs
$O(MK^2+K^3+K^2Q)$ and stores $O(MK+K^2+KQ)$.  Hence, when $M\asymp K$ and
$S,T,Q,d_h=O(1)$, the two paths scale as $O(K^2)$ and $O(K^3)$ respectively.
No distributional assumption is needed for this comparison.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The head cost is Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-prompt-head}.  A block HVP
on $Q$ vectors consists of $GV$ and $G^\top(GV)$, costing $O(MKQ)$, while the
low-rank preconditioner costs $O(KSQ)$.  Dense normal formation, Cholesky,
and triangular solution give the three classical terms.
\end{proof}

This asymptotic advantage is a setup statement, not a claim that an iterative
step beats a cached triangular solve.  A paired H100 benchmark with $M=4K$
confirms the distinction.  At $K=4096,Q=1$, dense setup plus solve takes
$17.519$ ms, while head plus HB--10, Chebyshev--10, and PCG--10 take $8.407$,
$8.113$, and $8.742$ ms.  With both geometries already cached, the dense solve
takes $0.874$ ms and Chebyshev--10 takes $3.630$ ms.  At $K=2048$ the dense
total still wins.  These are synthetic latency measurements with fixed stable
polynomial endpoints; they establish the crossover mechanism but not
large-$K$ PDE accuracy.

\paragraph{Elliptic PDE learning audit.}
The script
\texttt{audit\_pde\_matrix\_free\_learning.py} tests the missing PDE claim on
an exact uniformly elliptic one-dimensional weak-form law with
\(K=32\), state dimension \(64\), \(M=128\), \(S=4\), depth \(8\), three
training seeds, \(800\) training steps, and \(4096\) held-out tasks per seed.
With two block-power refinements, trained-head PCG--8 has mean relative
\(H\)-risk \(1.8113\times10^{-8}\).  A favorable head-free PCG--16 baseline,
which was originally charged only \(rS=8\) extra iterations, has risk
\(4.7393\times10^{-8}\).  The exact setup count is instead \(r+1=3\)
block-HVP rounds, or \((r+1)S=12\) sequential scalar-HVP equivalents, because
forming the projected operator requires one final block action.  Head plus
PCG--8 therefore uses eleven batched HVP rounds or twenty scalar equivalents;
head-free PCG--20 still has risk \(4.7393\times10^{-8}\) at the float32 floor.
The factor \(2.62\) is an error ratio, not a work-reduction factor; the
round-count reduction is \(20/11\simeq1.82\) when the block action is parallel.

This is \emph{not} evidence for learned softmax routing.  The initialized
head with the same two refinements has risk \(1.81125\times10^{-8}\), and its
mean overlap with the exact top-\(S\) eigenspace is \(0.9990\); training
changes neither quantity detectably.  The exact block-power/Ritz algebra,
not the learned score, explains the gain.  Moreover trained HB--8 has risk
\(6.88\times10^{-1}\), while exact-interval Chebyshev--8 has
\(7.79\times10^{-2}\).  Both are far from PCG on the same head.  The
effective condition number is only about \(34.4\), but \(25\) of the \(32\)
eigenvalues lie in one near-ridge cluster and the spectral effective rank is
\(5.99\); PCG exploits this cluster structure whereas interval minimax
polynomials discard it.

All converged PCG variants have essentially the same statistical query MSE,
\(8.33\times10^{-4}\), because their algebraic errors are already below the
posterior/statistical floor.  This audit therefore rejects the hypotheses
that HB is quasi-PCG on this PDE law and that the present softmax has
demonstrated useful learning.  It supports the cluster-aware moment
controller in Theorem~\ref{thm:risk-optimal-moment-polynomial}: on an
independent split, an oracle promptwise degree-eight polynomial fitted to the
unweighted effective spectral measure has spectral risk below \(10^{-15}\);
its reconstructed float32 iterate reaches \(1.73\times10^{-8}\), the same
numerical floor as PCG.  A single law-level polynomial reaches only about
\(10^{-5}\).  The next learned object must therefore predict promptwise
cluster statistics, not only global HB scalars or two support endpoints.

\paragraph{Learned spectral-measure Chebyshev audit.}
The follow-up script
\texttt{audit\_pde\_moment\_chebyshev\_learning.py} implements that object.
Only a 32-cluster spectral-measure MLP is trained; the matrix-free
preconditioner is frozen.  Seven prompt invariants determine ordered positive
nodes, normalized positive masses, and a conservative upper expansion.
Exact spectra supervise pretraining and validation only and are absent at
inference.  Coefficients come from the exact shifted-Chebyshev Gram solve and
the degree-eight iterate from exact Clenshaw.  The audit uses three seeds,
800 updates, 4096 validation prompts and 4096 fresh evaluation prompts per
seed on both the correlated and well-conditioned elliptic laws.

On the correlated law the learned polynomial has mean relative \(H\)-risk
\(9.00\times10^{-4}\) under the original trace-scaled frozen head, compared
with \(3.78\times10^{-1}\) for oracle-interval
Richardson, \(8.65\times10^{-1}\) for oracle-interval HB, and
\(7.79\times10^{-2}\) for oracle-interval Chebyshev.  Thus learning the
spectral masses, rather than only the support, gives an 86-fold improvement
over minimax Chebyshev at the same degree.  Nevertheless, same-head PCG--8
has \(1.81\times10^{-8}\), head-free PCG--8 has \(2.19\times10^{-6}\), and
head-free PCG--20 has \(4.74\times10^{-8}\).  A residual-guarded variant has
\(3.09\times10^{-7}\) but falls back on \(95.7\%\) of prompts, so it is not a
useful Chebyshev victory over PCG.

On the well-conditioned elliptic law the learned measure gives
\(7.44\times10^{-7}\), while all three exact-interval stationary methods are
already at the float32 floor near \(8\times10^{-14}\); there is no reason to
learn a more complicated controller there.  Across both laws the exact
spectral prediction and the realized Clenshaw risk agree to plotting
precision (respectively \(9.0015\times10^{-4}\) versus
\(9.0010\times10^{-4}\), and \(7.4433\times10^{-7}\) versus
\(7.4431\times10^{-7}\)).  Hence the learned object is a genuine in-context
spectral statistic rather than an MLP emulation of solver arithmetic, but the
experiment rejects superiority over PCG.  HB remains the simplest exact tied
loop; this audit initially made learned-measure Chebyshev the better
fixed-polynomial option on a clustered law, before the routed Krylov measure
below removed its MLP bottleneck.  PCG remains the accuracy baseline when
adaptive Krylov state is allowed.

The decisive paired rerun freezes instead the same tightly certified heads
used by the preceding HB/PCG PDE audit and trains the measure controller for
1600 updates.  At identical setup and depth, same-head PCG--8 has mean
relative \(H\)-risk \(1.8113\times10^{-8}\), trained HB--8 has
\(7.3392\times10^{-5}\), and learned-measure Chebyshev--8 has
\(1.5091\times10^{-4}\).  Thus HB is \(2.06\) times more accurate than the
currently learned fixed polynomial, whereas PCG is \(4.05\times10^3\) times
more accurate than HB.  This is an estimation gap, not a polynomial-capacity
gap: the prompt-ESD oracle polynomial reconstructed in float32 reaches
\(1.73\times10^{-8}\).  Moreover the spectral prediction
\(1.50896\times10^{-4}\) agrees with the realized Clenshaw risk
\(1.50913\times10^{-4}\), so the joint theory predicts the implemented
controller without asking the MLP to emulate solver arithmetic.  A residual
guard falls back to PCG on \(92.6\)--\(95.9\%\) of prompts and remains
\(18.8\) times worse than direct same-head PCG; it is not selected.

\paragraph{Routed block--Krylov measure audit.}
The controller in Proposition~\ref{prop:routed-krylov-measure} removes the
free spectral MLP.  On the same three frozen PDE heads, \(q=1\) gives mean
relative \(H\)-risk \(2.803\times10^{-3}\), whereas \(q=2\) gives
\(9.676\times10^{-6}\).  The latter is \(7.58\) times more accurate than
trained HB--8 and \(15.6\) times more accurate than learned-measure
Chebyshev--8.  Fine-tuning only the softmax head for 800 updates changes the
risk to \(9.945\times10^{-6}\), so the gain is caused by the exact Krylov
measure rather than additional learned routing.  Its total sequential work
is 28 scalar-HVP equivalents.  Head-free PCG--8 already has
\(2.192\times10^{-6}\), head-free PCG--28 has \(4.739\times10^{-8}\), and
same-head PCG--8 has \(1.812\times10^{-8}\); the structured polynomial does
not beat PCG.  \(q=3\) gives \(8.868\times10^{-6}\), only an \(8.35\%\)
reduction for four
additional scalar-HVP equivalents, and \(q=4\) is indistinguishable from
\(q=3\).  Thus \(q=2\) is the measured risk--work knee.

The exact-spectrum head admits a particularly simple commuting oracle limit.
It is useful for identifying what perfect spectral routing would achieve, but
it is not automatically the limit of the block-power matrix-free head in
Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-moment-interface}.  Suppose
$\nu_K\Rightarrow\nu$ on a compact subset
of $(0,\overline L]$, $S/K\to\sigma$, the empirical slot-query law converges
to $\pi$, and the score
$\ell(q,\lambda;\nu)=q^\top k_\theta(x(\lambda;\nu))/\sqrt{d_h}$ is bounded
and continuous.  Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Z(q)&=\int e^{\ell(q,t;\nu)}\,\nu(dt),\\
 g_{\theta,\sigma}(\lambda;\nu)
 &=1-\exp\!\left[-\sigma\int
 \frac{e^{\ell(q,\lambda;\nu)}}{Z(q)}\,\pi(dq)\right],\\
 \nu_{\rm eff}
 &=\bigl[(1-g_{\theta,\sigma})\lambda
       +g_{\theta,\sigma}\bigr]_{\#}\nu .
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:softmax-head-limit}
\end{equation}
This follows from the softmax law of large numbers and
$\log(1-\omega)=-\omega+o(K^{-1})$.  It requires neither Gaussian prompts,
isotropic eigenvectors, nor commutation with the physical Jacobian.  If $S$
is fixed, then $\sigma=0$ and a bounded-score head cannot change the bulk
law: it is a finite spectral/outlier correction.  A nontrivial bulk
preconditioner requires $S=\Theta(K)$ or a concentrating low-temperature
attention regime.  This scaling distinction is part of the prediction, not
an experimental tuning rule.

For the implemented matrix-free head, the corresponding asymptotic closure
is instead the limit of
\((K_r,J_r,L_r)\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-moment-interface} and of
the teacher/physical overlaps.  Equation~\eqref{eq:softmax-head-limit}
becomes exact for it only when the routed block converges to an invariant
spectral subspace (or under an additional asymptotic commutation theorem).

Combining \eqref{eq:softmax-head-limit} with
\eqref{eq:joint-hyperparameter-predictor} predicts
$(\theta,\sigma,\alpha,\beta,L)$ jointly.  With only support information it
uses the closed-form minimax HB pair; with the limiting weighted law it uses
the two-scalar distribution-aware risk.  Thus the head and HB coefficients
are not calibrated in two unrelated stages.

\section{Replica and random-matrix substitutions}

Let $\nu_\theta$ be the limiting joint spectral law of the encoded normal
operator and the fixed learned preconditioner.  For the actual matrix-free
head, the replica state must first determine
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\theta,r}
 =\left(\nu_H,K_r,J_r,L_r,
   \Gamma_{U,T},\Gamma_{U,b},\Gamma_{U,\mathrm{phys}}\right),
\]
where the last three terms denote the limiting cross measures or finite-rank
overlaps with the teacher, right-hand side, and physical metric.  The
preconditioner replica order parameters must include at least the
physical-space overlap and the observable-space overlap
\[
 m_T=\frac1r\tr(P_{\widehat T}P_T),
 \qquad
 m_S=\frac1s\tr(P_{Q_\theta}P_{S(G)}),
\]
together with the projected Ritz spectral law.  A full learned matrix
$W_{\rm dec}$ and a recovered projector $Q_\theta Q_\theta^\top$ are not the
same order parameter.  Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix-free-moment-interface}
then maps \(\mathcal Q_{\theta,r}\) deterministically to the fixed
preconditioner used by every recurrent block.

For a commuting model with isotropic query covariance, Richardson's filter is
replaced by
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal R_{\rm solve,L}^{\rm HB}
 &\longrightarrow
 \int \frac{p_L^{\rm HB}(p\widehat a)^2}{\widehat a^2}
 \,d\nu_\theta(\widehat a,p),\\
 \mathcal R_{\rm solve,L}^{\rm Ch}
 &\longrightarrow
 \int \frac{p_L^{\rm Ch}(p\widehat a)^2}{\widehat a^2}
 \,d\nu_\theta(\widehat a,p),
\end{align*}
up to the query-variance scale.  These substitutions are valid because the
coefficients are conditionally independent of the right-hand side.

For adaptive HB or Chebyshev, let $\xi(\widehat H,B_\theta)$ denote the finite
vector of fixed spectral summaries supplied to the interval MLP.  If $\xi$
self-averages under the replica saddle, then the predicted endpoints and hence
the HB pair or Chebyshev schedule converge to deterministic functions of the
existing spectral order parameters.  The corresponding integral above is then
used with that limiting polynomial.  Without self-averaging, the joint law
must be enlarged to $\nu_\theta(\widehat a,p,\xi)$ and the appropriate filter
is averaged conditionally on $\xi$:
\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm solve,L}^{\rm Ch}
 \longrightarrow
 \int \frac{p_L^{\rm Ch}(p\widehat a;h_\phi(\xi))^2}
 {\widehat a^2}
 \,d\nu_\theta(\widehat a,p,\xi).
\]
The same joint law can evaluate the sensitivity-weighted coverage failure in
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-coverage-failure}.  No layer-indexed Krylov order
parameters are needed: once $\xi$ is fixed, both HB and Chebyshev are fixed
polynomials independent of the right-hand side.

For the genuinely joint prediction, the replica saddle must also determine
the averaged bivariate measure and cross measure from
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-polynomial-risk}.  Let $q$ collect the encoder overlap,
preconditioner overlap, spectral law, and physical sensitivity order
parameters.  The required closure is
\[
 (\overline\Gamma_\theta,\overline\zeta_\theta,
 \mathcal R_{\rm enc}(\theta),T_s)
 \longrightarrow
 (\Gamma_q,\zeta_q,\mathcal R_{\rm enc}(q),T_s(q)).
\]
If hyperparameters affect representation learning, one must not optimize them
after solving a controller-independent encoder saddle.  Instead, with
$\Psi_{\rm tr}$ the replica training action, the predictive construction is
\begin{align}
 q_{s,L,\eta}
 &\in\operatorname*{extr}_q
 \Psi_{\rm tr}(q;s,L,\eta),
 \label{eq:controller-dependent-replica-saddle}\\
 (s^\star,L^\star,\eta^\star)
 &\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{s,L,\eta}
 \left\{\mathcal R_{s,L}^{\rm test}
 (q_{s,L,\eta},\eta)+\tau_{\rm hw}T_s(L,q_{s,L,\eta})\right\}.
 \label{eq:replica-hyperparameter-prediction}
\end{align}
The test risk in \eqref{eq:replica-hyperparameter-prediction} is obtained by
replacing the finite-task measures in \eqref{eq:exact-joint-risk} with
$(\Gamma_q,\zeta_q)$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:controller-dependent-replica-saddle}--
\eqref{eq:replica-hyperparameter-prediction} are the promised joint
encoder--decoder hyperparameter theory.  A frozen-encoder two-scalar HB fit is
the special case in which $q$ is fixed; the full theory predicts when that
modular approximation is valid through Proposition~\ref{prop:gradient-fidelity}.

There is no analogous fixed-filter substitution for PCG.  A PCG replica or
state evolution needs layer-indexed overlaps, for example residual norm,
preconditioned residual norm, direction norm, and their correlations with the
teacher, together with the scalar ratios $\alpha_\ell,\beta_\ell$.  Treating
PCG as Richardson with a better deterministic polynomial is incorrect.

\section{Optimizer boundary: AdamW versus Muon}

The collapsed gradient-flow ODEs previously derived for a factorized linear
attention encoder describe Euclidean gradient flow (and, approximately, small
step SGD/Adam regimes).  Muon replaces a matrix momentum update by an
approximately orthogonalized Newton--Schulz update.  Hence it changes the
training trajectory and implicit bias; the old ODE and its plateau times
cannot be reused.

The hypothesis class and a uniform generalization bound are optimizer-agnostic
once a parameter set is fixed, but the replica-selected saddle and dynamical
order parameters can depend on the optimizer.  Consequently Muon remains an
empirical ablation on large hidden encoder matrices.  It is not included in
the present replica claim, and AdamW/gradient flow remains the theoretical
reference.
